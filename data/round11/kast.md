## Completeness summary

**Complete from the Lick manuals and the Berkeley supernova papers:** the Cassegrain plate scale (3.86 arcsec/mm), the slits (0.4-145 arcsec, 145 arcsec long), the dichroic (D55, cross-over about 5500 A, blue reflected, red transmitted, in the diverging beam after the common slit), the two off-axis paraboloid collimators (f = 1416 mm, beam 82.6 mm), the 188.7 mm all-refractive cameras, both CCDs (blue Fairchild 2048 x 2048, red Hamamatsu 1024 x 4096, 15 um pixels), the grism and grating tables (600/4310 blue at 1.02 A/px, 300/7500 red at 2.55 A/px, with coverage), the pixel scale (0.43 arcsec/px) and the supernova program's typical setup and resolution (about 6 A blue and 11 A red FWHM at a 2 arcsec slit).

**Gaps that matter:** (1) the grisms' prism angle and glass are not published: the blue apex (33.90 deg in fused silica) is EFFECTIVE, fitted to the straight-through wavelength 4340 A, with the glass chosen from the three published grism dispersions; (2) the camera-collimator angle and the gratings' incidence are not published: psi = 45 deg is EFFECTIVE (the dispersion depends on it by only about 2% over 10-50 deg); (3) the only layout is one oblique 3-D drawing (TR66 p. 7): the dichroic angle (32.5 deg), the collimator tilt (5.0 deg) and the red collimator-to-grating distance (1070 mm) are MEASURED from it with a scale from the collimator focal length, +-25%; (4) the Cassegrain focal ratio is not stated (F = 17.53, DERIVED), and the sourced 82.6 mm beam disagrees with it by 2% (Conflicts); (5) no instrument paper gives a measured R from arc lines: the validation targets are the Berkeley papers' round resolutions and the manual's ranges.

**Status:** in operation since early 1992 (the Lick pages were updated in July 2025). No '(design)' tag. The design paper named in the task (Miller & Stone 1993) is the Lick Technical Report No. 66, posted by Lick as a September 1994 scan; no ADS entry exists for it.

## Optical layout (from the published figure)

Source: TR66 p. 7 (PDF p. 13), the 3-D drawing of the optical layout (also the Lick User's Guide's Hardware Overview figure, FIG); rendered at 150 dpi and rotated upright for measurement. The figure has no scale; the known dimension is the collimator focal length. Sketch (not to scale; the telescope axis runs down the page):

```
            telescope beam
                 |
          decker/slit, filter wheels             gratings -> red camera -> red CCD
                 |                                  ^  (beam up and right of the axis)
      dichroic (AOI ~32 deg, blue reflected) ---> blue collimator (upper left)
                 |                                          |
                 v                                          v (beam down and right)
        red collimator (f = 1416 mm)             blue filter tray, grism, camera, blue CCD
```

- **Source figure** (SOURCED, TR66 p. i (PDF p. 5)): TR66 p. 7 (PDF p. 13), the 3-D drawing of the optical layout by Tony Misch, labeled 'Kast Double Spectrograph' (an oblique perspective with no scale bar); the Lick User's Guide redraws the same view (FIG, letter labels a-r). No plan view, ray trace or dimensioned drawing is published in any saved document. Everything below is read from this one figure, rotated upright, at 150 dpi (1650 x 1275 px; coordinates are (x, y) in that render, y down).  Quote: "We sincerely thank Tony Misch for his superb 3-D drawing of the optical layout of the Kast spectrograph."
- **Element order** (SOURCED, UMO Hardware Overview): The telescope beam comes down a vertical line at x = 752: decker and slit (y = 290-322), the three filter wheels (y = 330-390), the two dichroic/mirror plates #2 and #1 (y = 600-640), then the red collimator at the foot of the line (752, 925). The dichroic transmits the red straight on to the red collimator; the red collimator returns the beam up and to the right to the three gratings (y = 430-490, beside the filter wheels), and the beam goes on to the red filter turret, red camera, shutter and dewar at the right. The blue light is reflected by the dichroic up and to the left to the blue collimator at (395, 455); its returning beam runs down and to the right, past the dichroic plates, to the blue filter tray, the three grisms, the blue camera, shutter and dewar at the lower right (grisms at (1040, 790)).  Quote: "On the red side, after striking a silvered collimator, the beam proceeds to a reflectance grating and then passes through a filter wheel"
- **Scale of the figure** (MEASURED, FIG TR66 p. 7): Known dimension: the slit stands at each collimator's focus, so the slit-to-red-collimator distance is f_col = 1416 mm (SOURCED, INFERRED that the slit is at the focus). In the figure that distance runs from the slit (752, 322) to the collimator (752, 925): 603 px, so 2.35 mm/px along the vertical (the telescope axis, which an oblique projection draws near true length). Used for lengths along that axis only; horizontal and diagonal distances are foreshortened by an unknown factor.
- **Slit to dichroic** (MEASURED, FIG TR66 p. 7): The plates stand at y about 610: 288 px = 680 mm from the slit (48% of the way to the red collimator), then 315 px = 740 mm to the red collimator. The blue collimator is the same 1416 mm from the slit by the dichroic's reflection, so it stands 1416 - 680 = 736 mm from the dichroic (the figure draws 376 px, 880 mm, but that line is diagonal and foreshortened by an unknown amount).
- **Dichroic angle** (MEASURED, FIG TR66 p. 7): The reflected blue beam leaves the plates at (735, 615) toward the blue collimator at (395, 455): a turn of about 115 deg from the downward chief ray, an angle of incidence of 32.5 deg (+-8 deg, an oblique view). No document states it. The two plates (#1 and #2) are drawn as parallel flats facing the same way.
- **Red collimator to grating (layLen, red)** (MEASURED, FIG TR66 p. 7): The red collimator (752, 925) to the grating carrier (825, 478): 454 px, 1070 mm at the scale above (the beam is nearly vertical, so little foreshortening). The grating stands near the level of the filter wheels, as in LRIS, not at the pupil image of the secondary. Entered layLen = 1070 mm.
- **Collimator tilt (colTilt)** (MEASURED, FIG TR66 p. 7): The returning red beam leaves the collimator at 10.1 deg to the incoming chief ray (lateral 80 px in 447 px of vertical travel): an off-axis paraboloid whose parent axis is tilted 5.0 deg from the incoming ray. Entered colTilt = 5.0 for both arms (the blue collimator is 'identical', TR66 p. 6; its two beam lines at the blue collimator are drawn 1.5 deg apart in the projection, too unreliable to use, so the blue value is SCHEMATIC).
- **Gratings to camera, camera-collimator angle** (SCHEMATIC, FIG TR66 p. 7): The gratings (three in a tray, one used at a time, tilted by remote control) turn the beam to the camera at the right; the projected turn is about 100 deg, which is a projection artifact. The explorer's psi = 45 deg is EFFECTIVE (Parameters). The disperser-to-camera distance (camLen) is NOT drawn to scale and is left at the explorer's default.
- **Blue arm** (SCHEMATIC, UMB Grisms): Blue collimator, filter tray, one of three grisms (all always in place) and the camera stand in a straight line (the grism passes the straight-through wavelength undeviated): the blue camera axis lies on the collimator axis. layLen for the blue arm is entered as 1070 mm like the red (SCHEMATIC: the blue line is diagonal and the figure has no scale along it).  Quote: "We have three grisms and they are all always in place"
- **Verdict: collimator on- or off-axis** (SOURCED, UMR Collimator): OFF-AXIS (SOURCED text 'off-axis paraboloid' for both arms; the figure shows the returning beam beside, not along, the incoming one, with the slit and filter wheels outside the returning beam).  Quote: "The off-axis paraboloid is silvered and overcoated."

## Conflicts between sources

- Beam size and focal ratio: UMQ and TR66 give a 82.6 mm beam on the 1416 mm collimator (F = 17.14), but the 3.86 arcsec/mm plate scale on the 3.048 m primary gives F = 17.53 and an 80.77 mm beam (and f/17.14 would need a 3.117 m pupil). The record keeps the sourced plate scale and focal length (so the pixel scale and dispersions come out as published) and takes the explorer's beam from F = 17.53. A larger secondary obstruction does not resolve it (that makes the pupil smaller, not larger). Possibly 82.6 mm is the design beam with margin.
- Citation year: the Lick-posted report is dated September 1994; BSNIP I, III and the data-release paper cite 'Miller & Stone 1993'; the User's Guide is not dated. The record cites the report as posted (TR66, 1994).
- Red coverage: the grating table gives 6785 A for the 300/7500 (2661 px at 2.55 A/px) but the detector page says the spectrum covers rows 760 to 3310 (2551 px, 6505 A). The record uses 2551 rows, so the model's 6489 A is 4.4% below the table. The 2016 news says the single-image coverage grew 'by about 20%' (6785 / 5520 = +23%).
- D55 transition width: the manual says 'About 200 angstroms of the spectrum are affected'; the measured D55 curve (Fig. 3) has a 10-90% width of 380 A (reflectance 0.90 at 5225 A, 0.10 at 5608 A). Entered 380 A (MEASURED). The same page says the crossover is 'about 5500 A'; the curve crosses at 5450 A.
- Lick's synopsis page gives the blue grism ranges as 3100-5520 A (600/4310) and 3100-6200 A (452/3306); the Quick Reference and Blue Side pages give 3300-5520 and 3150-6200 A. The record uses the User's Guide values.
- Standard slit: the Lick manual's one stated default is 0.5 arcsec (arc lamps); the Berkeley supernova program's typical slit is 2 arcsec. The preset uses 2 arcsec, which also makes the published 6 A and 11 A resolutions the targets.

## Notes

- Era and status. Kast has run since early 1992 and is in operation (the Lick pages were updated July 2025). The preset represents the current configuration: the blue Fairchild 2048 x 2048 CCD (since September 2008) and the red Hamamatsu 1024 x 4096 CCD (since 18 September 2016), both 15 um pixels. The 1994 report describes the Reticon era: two 1200 x 400 CCDs of 27 um pixels (0.78 arcsec/px, 2.35 A/px for the 600/mm gratings); its tables and tilt formulas are for that era and are not used.
- Two physical spectrographs in one housing. Each arm has its own off-axis paraboloid collimator (1416 mm), its own camera (188.7 mm lens) and CCD; the dichroic sits in the diverging beam after the common slit (All-arms group 'Kast', split 'focus', slit shared, relay none). The polarimeter module (waveplate plus calcite) can replace the user filter wheels; it is not modeled.
- Explorer representation. mode grating; D 3.048 m; F 17.53 (DERIVED); W 80.77 mm; fcol 1416 mm; fcam 188.7 mm; slit 2.0 arcsec; nObj 1; colTilt 5.0 deg (MEASURED from the figure; the blue value SCHEMATIC); layLen 1070 mm (red MEASURED, blue SCHEMATIC); cam lens; Nx x Ny 2048 x 2048 (blue) and 2551 x 1024 (red), pix 15 um. Blue: gType grism, G 600, gKind ruled, gGlass silica, gA 33.90 deg (EFFECTIVE), lamC 4340 A (the straight-through wavelength, fixed by the grism). Red: gType refl, G 300, lamB 7500 A, lamC 7800 A (INFERRED), psi 45 deg (EFFECTIVE).
- What the explorer cannot represent: the grism's blaze (a grism of a given apex only fixes the straight-through wavelength), the moveable detector stage (blue x-stage shifts the coverage by 100-200 A), the camera's measured aberrations and flexure (up to 9 px), the dichroic's ghosts and the 10 px shift of the red spectrum, the polarimeter, and the red camera's loss of focus off the 4000-11000 A design range.
- The 2016 dichroics: a new D46 (4600 A) and a D57 (5700 A) with sharper transitions and brighter ghosts are available; only the D55 (the supernova standard) is entered.
