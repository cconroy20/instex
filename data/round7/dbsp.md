## Completeness summary

**Feed**: Hale 5.08 m Cassegrain f/16 (hale.md: 81.28 m, 2.538 arcsec/mm); the spectrograph's own documents give f/15.7 with 2.5 arcsec/mm (OG82) and 2.57 arcsec/mm at the slit (PDS = f/15.80); the collimator (2311 mm, 146 mm beam) accepts f/15.83. Recommended entry: F = 15.83, W = 146 mm, which keeps f_col = 2311 mm and the published 0.389 / 0.293 arcsec per pixel. Slit 128 arcsec long, 0.5-10 arcsec wide (OG82: 125 arcsec, 0.5/1/2). One spectrum per arm.

**Optics (complete from OG82 plus the Palomar pages)**: a dichroic at 45 deg immediately behind the slit reflects the blue and transmits the red; each side has its own field lens and prism block, filter wheel, Cassegrain collimator (2311 mm, 146 mm beam, on-axis), grating (154 x 206 mm; 8 gratings usable on either side), camera and CCD. Blue camera: folded f/1.5 Schmidt, 229 mm (PDS 9 in), 38.33 deg (OG82) or 38.50 deg (POV, PypeIt) from the collimator. Red camera: a 152 mm f/1.0 Schmidt in 1982; since 1995 a seven-element Epps lens camera, 12 in = 304.8 mm, 35.0 deg. Detectors now: e2v CCD 23 (2048 x 4096, 15 um, 0.389 arcsec/px) and LBNL (4096 x 2048, 15 um, 0.293 arcsec/px); in 1982 an RCA 320 x 512 (30 um) and a TI 800 x 800 (15 um).

**Dispersion**: the PDS and POV grating tables (A/mm) are reproduced by the explorer's geometry to 1 percent for 158/7500, 316/7500, 600/4000, 300/3990 and the 1200s (DERIVED), and C22 confirms 1.1 A/px blue and 1.5 A/px red for the 600/4000 + 316/7500 pair. The POV blaze column is the PDS blaze shifted by cos(psi/2) for four gratings (INFERRED).

**Layout (figure-derived)**: OG82 Fig. 1 (PDF p. 4) and the POV red-side raytrace give the element order and the arm geometry: slit, dichroic (blue reflected left), field-lens block (blue: 90-degree turn; red: parallelogram shift of 97 mm to the right), filter, annular collimator primary with the beam through its hole, secondary on a quartz plate 390 mm beyond (MEASURED), grating 490 mm beyond the secondary (SOURCED), camera swinging out of the page (opposite senses for the two arms). Collimator: ON-AXIS (INFERRED from "no larger than the shadow of the prime-focus cage in the collimated beam" and the holes in the primaries).

**Gaps**: the red lens camera's prescription and f/ratio (only 12 in); the camera-to-grating distances; the blue camera's current state ("has changed"); measured resolving powers (only arc FWHM in pixels, 3.0 and 2.7, for a 0.5 arcsec slit); which camera swings into and which out of the page; transition widths of D48 and D68 (no curves); the Palomar grating calculator (the CGI is gone: 404).

## Optical layout (from the published figures)

Figures: OG82 Fig. 1 (PDF p. 4; printed p. 589: the upper part: slit, dichroic, field lenses, separating prisms, filters F, Cassegrain collimators, gratings below), OG82 Fig. 2 (the blue f/1.5 folded Schmidt), OG82 Fig. 6 (PDF p. 9; the instrument on its cart); POV figures RTF ("Raytrace of DBSP Red": slit/focal plane, dichroic seen edge-on, prism, collimator primary and secondary, grating, camera of lenses, detector; "off-axis field points are +/-1 arcmin") and PGD (grating geometry). Scales: Fig. 1 has no scale bar; the 146 mm collimated beam (rim rays 233 px apart in a 200-dpi crop; 0.627 mm/px) is the known dimension (its outer rays converge to the hole in the secondary). Cross-check: the hole in the primary measures 54 mm = 0.37 of the beam, the 0.36 shadow of the 72-inch cage (OG82's own statement), so the scale is right to a few percent. RTF's scale is 2.16 mm/px from its beam (67.5 px) and 2.06 mm/px from the grating's length.

Element order of one arm (light travels down in Fig. 1): slit (Cassegrain focal plane) - dichroic (45 deg) - field-lens block with a 45-degree fold (blue: left, one fold; red: right, two folds in a rhomboid) - filter wheel F - hole in the annular collimator primary - secondary on a transparent quartz plate on the axis (390 mm beyond the primary) - back to the primary - collimated 146 mm beam through the transparent plate - grating (about 490 mm beyond the plate; rotates about an axis in the plane of Fig. 1) - camera, swung out of the plane of Fig. 1 by 38.5 deg (blue) or 35.0 deg (red) from the collimator axis.

Collimator verdict: **ON-AXIS** (a coaxial Cassegrain). Evidence: OG82 Sec. III.A: "This allows the secondaries to be no larger than the shadow of the prime-focus cage in the collimated beam, thus eliminating any vignetting" (the secondary is in the middle of the collimated beam); POV Sec. 4.b: the light enters "through holes in the collimator primaries"; Fig. 1 and RTF draw the arriving beam passing through the primary's hole and the secondary on the axis, with nothing tilted. The slit is not in the returning beam (the dichroic and prism blocks move the beam to the collimator's hole): the explorer's `colTilt: 0`, with a drawing that cannot show the second mirror.

```js
  shared: { top: ["D"], m: ["slitW", "nObj"] },
  note: "DBSP's one slit stands at the Cassegrain focus; the dichroic follows it in the diverging beam and each arm's field lens and prism block feed its Cassegrain collimator",
  tree: { cut: 5500, w: 320, aoi: 45, src: "OG82 Sec. II.E (45 deg, behind the slit); PDS/POV (D55); D55 curve (FIGURE READ-OFF)",

    T: { fold: 45, rel: -1, src: "OG82 Fig. 1 (red rhomboid, first face)", to: { fold: 45, rel: 1, src: "OG82 Fig. 1 (red rhomboid, second face)", to: "dbspr" } } } }
```

- **Split kind**: `"focus"` with `slit: "shared"`, exactly as MIKE (one slit at the focus, the dichroic right behind it, then each arm's optics). `"preslit"` does not fit (that kind puts the dichroic in a collimated beam ahead of the slits, as UVES does); `"collimated"` does not fit (DBSP has two collimators).
- **Dichroic**: cut 5500 A (D55, the recommended dichroic; D52 would be 5200, D68 6800, D48 4800), AOI 45 deg (SOURCED), transition width w = 320 A in MIKE's convention (80 percent reflected to 80 percent transmitted: 5319 to 5637 A; FIGURE READ-OFF from d55.gif; D52: 453 A). Position: SOURCED, immediately behind the slit; MEASURED 72 mm along the beam from the slit, the dichroic 93 mm long.
- **Turning senses (OG82 Fig. 1, slit at the top, beam traveling down the page)**: at the dichroic the BLUE beam turns 90 deg clockwise (down to left); the blue prism block turns it back 90 deg counterclockwise (left to down), after about 100 mm, and it then runs about 144 mm to the collimator. The RED beam goes straight through, about 85 mm, into a parallelogram block that shifts it 97 mm to the right (down to right to down) and runs 72 mm to its collimator. In the tree's convention (a fold's `rel` is relative to the last dichroic's turn): blue fold `rel: -1`; red first fold `rel: -1` (the side opposite to the blue turn), second fold `rel: +1`. The two collimators' axes are 199 mm apart (MEASURED).
- **Grating senses**: the gratings rotate about axes in the plane of Fig. 1, so each camera swings OUT of that plane, one into the page and the other out of it: the two arms' camera turns are opposite (`af` +1 for one arm and -1 for the other); which is which is NOT FOUND. In the schematic layout of arms.js the senses are free.
- **Collimator in the arms drawing**: `col: "mirror"` with `colTilt: 0` in a `"focus"` split with a shared slit is a combination no existing group uses (MIKE, UVES, X-Shooter have lens collimators); in `unit()` the mirror branch always folds the beam RET off the slit's axis (the tele branch ignores colTilt), so the drawing will show a tilted single mirror. DBSP's real collimator is an on-axis folded Cassegrain (above); this is a drawing limitation, not a number.
- **Field of the dichroic**: the arms drawing draws one lens per arm (`relay`); DBSP has a field lens cemented to a prism block, and the dichroic's long dimension is 93 mm.
- **Shared controls**: the telescope, the slit width and nObj (one slit, one spectrum), like MIKE; each arm keeps its own grating and camera.

## Conflicts between sources

- **Cassegrain focal ratio and scale**: f/15.7 and 2.5 arcsec/mm (OG82, mutually inconsistent: f/15.7 on 5080 mm is 2.586 arcsec/mm; 2.5 is f/16.24) vs 2.57 arcsec/mm at the slit (PDS, f/15.80) vs f/16 and 2.538 arcsec/mm (hale.md from the 3200-inch focal length) vs the collimator's 2311.4 / 146 = f/15.83 (2.565 arcsec/mm). The recommended entry takes F = 15.83 so that the collimator and both pixel scales are right (0.3890 and 0.2917 arcsec/px against 0.389 and 0.293).
- **Blue camera-collimator angle**: 38 deg 20 arcmin = 38.333 deg (OG82 Sec. III.B) vs 38.50 deg (POV Sec. 4.c; PypeIt). The recommended entry uses 38.5 (current docs); the dispersion changes by 0.15 percent.
- **Blaze wavelengths**: PDS (7500, 3990, 7500, 4000, 10000, 5000, 7100, 9400 for 158, 300, 316, 600b, 600r, 1200b, 1200, 1200) vs POV (7560, 3990, 7150, 3780, 9500, 4700, 7100, 9400). Four of the differences are the cos(psi/2) shift of the blaze peak (INFERRED, above); 158 (7500 vs 7560; K11 also 7560) is not. The explorer takes the Littrow blaze from PDS (7500 for the 158).
- **Dispersions that differ by one unit**: 158/7500 red 202 (PDS) vs 201 (POV); 158 second order blue 134 vs 135; 1200 blue 36 vs 36 and 35 (9400); 600 blue 72 vs 71. The model gives 201.5, 71.3 and 35-36 (DERIVED).
- **Slit**: 125 arcsec long, 0.5/1.0/2.0 wide plus seven apertures (OG82, 1982) vs 128 arcsec, eight widths 0.5-10 (PDS, POV). The preset uses the current slit.
- **Red camera**: Schmidt f/1.0, 152 mm, scale reduction 15.2, 35 deg 00 arcmin, 178 mm corrector (OG82) vs a 12 in seven-element lens camera (since 1995; PDS, POV, K11). Reduction now 7.6. The 1982 instrument is recorded alongside; the preset uses the current camera.
- **Wavelength ranges**: 1982 design: blue 3200-5200 A, red 5200-10,000 A, usable to 10,400 A (OG82) vs now blue < 3000-7000 A and red 4700-11000 A for the instrument (PDS); per exposure the chip and dichroic set the range (a D55 pair covers ~3200-5500 A blue and 5500-11,000 A red).
