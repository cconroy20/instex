# Imager preset: Palomar 200-inch Hale / 4-Shooter -- sourced optical parameters

Compiled 2026-10-19 for the instex IMAGER mode, in the format of `data/imaging/widefield.md`. Model: Cassegrain focus (D, F_tel) -> four-faceted pyramid at the focal plane -> for each quadrant an ideal collimator f_col (= F_tel x W) -> collimated beam / pupil of diameter W -> ideal camera f_cam -> CCD; effective focal ratio f_cam / W; pixel scale = 206265 x p / (D x F_tel x f_cam / f_col). Every number comes from a document saved unmodified under `rawdata/imaging/fourshooter/` (or an existing `rawdata/presets/` file, keys OG82 and PDS reused from the long-slit round); each value carries its source key, location and a short verbatim quote. **DERIVED** marks arithmetic done here (shown). Quotes (102 including every ALT quote; 0 failures) were machine-checked against the extracted text of the saved files: pdftotext (-layout and -raw) for the PDFs, whose ADS-scan text layers are themselves OCR; tesseract 5.5.1 OCR (300-400 dpi page images) for the three scans without a text layer (P84, G84, B61) and for CPM86 pp. 348-349 and S86 p. 992; a tag-stripped pass for HTML; the decoded ADS record for G87OE. Glyphs were normalised (Unicode NFKC, curly quotes, primes, dashes, minus, multiplication and tilde signs); whitespace was collapsed and a piece may also match after joining hyphenated line breaks or with all spaces removed; ' ... ' joins non-adjacent pieces. OCR glyph errors are kept verbatim inside quotes and decoded in the notes ('0"334' = 0.334", 'fxm' or 'wu' = um, '//15.7' = f/15.7, '^' = ~, 'Â' = Angstrom). Orientation: north up, east left, as in the cited figures; quadrant numbering is the observers' CCD 1-4. Machine-readable twin: `data/imaging/fourshooter.json` (adds `preset`).

## Completeness summary

**Palomar 200-inch Hale / 4-Shooter**: telescope, splitter placement, detectors, layout, pixel scale and field complete; the reimaging optics are only partly published. The 4-Shooter instrument paper (Gunn et al. 1987, Optical Engineering 26, 779) could not be downloaded (SPIE refused the request), so its ADS abstract is the only part used; the brief's 'PASP 99, 518' is a different paper (the Palomar Dewar + CCD electronics, Gunn, Emory, Harris & Oke 1987), which itself cites the Optical Engineering paper for the 4-Shooter. The optics come from contemporaneous user papers (Porter 1984 specification sheet; Schneider et al. 1985, 1986, 1994; Campbell et al. 1986; Cohen 1986; Zaritsky et al. 1987; Dressler & Gunn 1992; Neuschaefer & Windhorst 1995; Postman et al. 1996) and the telescope from Bowen (1961) and Palomar's Hale page. D = 5.08 m (200 in), eps = 0.36 DERIVED (72-in prime-focus cage, which holds the Cassegrain secondary), Cassegrain f/16 (f = 81,280 mm; f/15.7 in OG82), primary f/3.3. A four-faceted reflecting pyramid sits at the Cassegrain focal plane (angle of incidence ~5 deg) and returns each quadrant toward the telescope into its own reimager: collimated beam, 4-in filter (two wheels ~1 m above the pyramid), small Schmidt camera, f/1.8 at the CCD (f/1.818 DERIVED; f_cam/f_col = 0.1136 DERIVED). Detectors: four TI 800 x 800, 15 um, 2 x 2 contiguous on the sky (1 NE, 4 NW, 2 SE, 3 SW), no sky gap by design, ~1" unusable crack at the facet edges and 20 dark px along the two apex edges of each chip. Validation: 0.335"/px; 8'56" x 8'56" (1600 x 1600 px; 8.9'). Gaps: collimator and camera focal lengths, pupil diameter and position, pyramid facet figure and size, a 4-Shooter-era obscuration measurement, later upgrades / decommissioning date (none found).

### Notes

- Explorer mapping (pyramid splitter): telescope Cassegrain, D = 5.08 m, eps = 0.36, F = 16 (f = 81,280 mm), primary f/3.3. Pyramid at the f/16 focal plane, four facets, one per field quadrant, angle of incidence ~5 deg -> each quadrant reflected back up ~10 deg off axis (DERIVED) into its own reimager: collimator (f_col, W not published) -> filter (4-in, collimated beam) -> small Schmidt camera (f/1.8 published; f/1.818 DERIVED) -> TI 800 x 800, 15 um CCD. Each chip sees 4.47' (800 px x 0.335"); the four sky quadrants abut in a 2 x 2 square 8.93' on a side with a ~1" unusable crack along the cross; 20 px along the two apex edges of each chip are dark (pyramid shadow).
- Drawing placeholder (NOT a published design value): f_col = 1000 mm (the 'about a meter' pyramid-to-filter distance, S94) -> W = 1000 / 16 = 62.5 mm, f_cam = 0.1136 x 1000 = 113.6 mm (f_cam / W = 1.818); only the ratio f_cam / f_col affects the pixel scale.
- Heritage: the splitter is the focal-plane image splitter developed for Hubble's Wide Field/Planetary Camera (G84, CPM86), and the CCDs are the TI chips built for WF/PC (P84); the predecessor was Gunn & Westphal's prime-focus PFUEI (P84: the 4-Shooter's 'simpler optical system with better coatings' vs PFUEI's 22 glass/air surfaces).
- Other modes: an auxiliary long-slit spectrograph behind a slit cut into one pyramid facet (S86: 1.5" x 2.0', Cassegrain Schmidt camera f/1.33 in a 3-in beam, 0.426"/px; the slit shows as a diagonal line in direct images, S91), simultaneous imaging on the other quadrants (S86), slitless grisms in front of all four CCDs, and drift-scan (transit) imaging with the CCD columns aligned east-west, triple-clocked (S94; G87OE 'scanning mode').
- Preset configuration: the direct-imaging Cassegrain 4-Shooter as described 1984-1996 (no later upgrade found). Unbinned 0.335"/px; N95 resampled 3 x 3 px into 2 x 2 (0.502"/px) for its catalogs.
- Quote-check caveats: all journal sources are ADS page scans; their text layers are OCR and carry glyph errors that are kept verbatim in the quotes (e.g. '0"334' = 0.334", 'fxm'/'wu' = um, '//15.7' = f/15.7, '^' = ~, 'Â' = Angstrom). The three scans without a text layer (P84, G84, B61) and the pages CPM86 pp. 348-349 and S86 p. 992 were OCR'd here with tesseract 5.5.1.
