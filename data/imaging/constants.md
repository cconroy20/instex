# IMAGER mode: reference constants (band menu, Moon size, seeing Moffat beta, Airy FWHM)

Compiled 2026-10-19 for the instex IMAGER mode, in the format of `data/round4/mosfire.md`. Every number comes from a document saved unmodified under `rawdata/imaging/constants/` (SVO pages and VOTables under `svo/`, papers under `papers/`, the NASA Moon fact sheet under `moon/`, IRAF help/parameter files under `iraf/`, the telescope-optics.net page under `airy/`); each value carries its source key, location and a short verbatim quote. **DERIVED** marks arithmetic done here (shown). All 225 quote pieces (including double-quoted fragments inside notes) were machine-checked against the extracted text of the saved files (pdftotext layout/raw/default; tag-stripped HTML including tooltip attributes), with glyphs normalised (primes, curly quotes, dashes and minus signs, the tilde operator, mu) and whitespace collapsed; ' ... ' joins non-adjacent pieces; table rows are quoted as whitespace-separated cells; ADS-scan OCR text is quoted as OCR'd. Machine-readable twin: `data/imaging/constants.json` (`band_menu` holds the ready-to-use menu).

## Completeness summary

**Band menu**: complete. One definition for all 16 bands: the SVO pivot wavelength (Calculated column), plus SVO's FWHM, W_eff and lambda_cen (half-maximum edges = lambda_cen -/+ FWHM/2, DERIVED) for the tooltip. Curves: Bessell (1990) UBVRI (energy-counter), SDSS 2.5 m u g r i z full response (DR7), Pan-STARRS1 y (Tonry et al. 2012; LSST/Rubin y as the alternative), 2MASS J H Ks (Cohen et al. 2003), MKO L' M' (IRTF NSFCam curves of the Tokunaga et al. 2002 filter set). Every SVO number was recomputed from the saved transmission curve (DERIVED, agreement <= 0.02 A), and each band is cross-checked against its primary paper (BM12, F96, D10, T12, C03, T02, TV05).

**Moon**: mean apparent diameter 1896" (31.6') quoted from the NSSDC fact sheet (mean value at opposition, distance 378,000 km); geocentric mean 1865" (31.1') and range 1761"-2008" (29.35'-33.46') are DERIVED from NSSDC distances and radius. No saved source states the range in arcsec.

**Moffat beta**: Kolmogorov seeing beta = 4.765 (Trujillo et al. 2001, Sec. 2.4; reproduced here as 4.73-4.78, DERIVED). Common alternative beta = 2.5 (T01: "the default value of the IRAF package"; IRAF DAOPHOT `moffat25`).

**Airy FWHM**: unobstructed 1.029 lambda/D quoted (Robertson & Bland-Hawthorn 2012) and DERIVED (1.02899). Obstructed FWHM(eps): **NOT FOUND** as numbers in any saved source; a DERIVED table from the annular-pupil formula is given, validated against Mahajan's first-dark-ring radii (as tabulated on telescope-optics.net).

## 1. Band menu (one wavelength per band, used only for lambda/D)

**Definition adopted**: lambda_pivot (SVO "λ pivot", Calculated column) for every band. One definition across all 16 bands. (1) The pivot wavelength depends on the passband only (no source spectrum), and Bessell & Murphy (2012) recommend it for describing a filter system. (2) SVO adopts it as each filter's reference wavelength (lambda_ref = lambda_pivot). (3) Recomputing from the saved curves shows SVO evaluates the pivot on the photon-counting response for both detector types (curve as given for photon counters, T/lambda for the energy-counter Bessell and NSFCam curves), so the same definition applies to every row. SVO's lambda_mean is instead computed on the energy-counting response; it differs from the pivot by +0.04% (M') to +1.35% (R), which does not matter for lambda/D.

Definition quotes:

- BM12, Appendix A.2.1: "We recommend the retention of only two, the pivot wavelength, λp , that is a property of the passband only, and the isophotal wavelength"
- BM12, Appendix A.2.1: "The pivot wavelength should be used as part of a description of the filter system"
- SVO:SDSS.u, VOTable, PARAM WavelengthRef <DESCRIPTION>: "Reference wavelength. Defined as the same than the pivot wavelength."
- SVO:SDSS.u, VOTable, PARAM WavelengthPivot <DESCRIPTION> (SVO labels this description "Peak wavelength"; the formula is the pivot): "Defined as sqrt{integ[x*filter(x) dx]/integ[filter(x) dx/x]}"
- SVO:SDSS.u, VOTable, PARAM WavelengthMean <DESCRIPTION>: "Mean wavelength. Defined as integ[x*filter(x) dx]/integ[filter(x) dx]"
- SVO:SDSS.u, VOTable, PARAM FWHM <DESCRIPTION>: "Full width at half maximum. Defined as the difference between the two wavelengths for which filter transmission is half maximum"
- SVO:SDSS.u, VOTable, PARAM WidthEff <DESCRIPTION>: "Equivalent to the horizontal size of a rectangle with height equal to maximum transmission and with the same area that the one covered by the filter"
- SVO:SDSS.u, browse page, tooltip of the λ mean / λ cen / FWHM properties: "Filter transmission is transformed to "energy counter" for these calculations, that is, the photon count transmission is multiplied by λ and normalized so that peak transmission is equal to 1."
- SVO:SDSS.u, VOTable, PARAM DetectorType <DESCRIPTION>: "Detector type. 0:Energy counter, 1:Photon counter."

**Caveat**: The SVO tooltip text quoted above also appears under λ pivot, but the saved curves show the pivot is NOT evaluated on the energy-normalised curve: SVO's pivot equals sqrt(int lambda T_ph / int T_ph/lambda) with T_ph the photon-counting response (agreement to the 0.01 A printed, all 16 bands, DERIVED), whereas lambda_mean, lambda_cen, FWHM and W_eff do use the energy-counting response.

### Conflicts between sources

- **Which wavelength.** Within a band, published 'central' wavelengths differ by up to ~2% depending on definition (pivot, mean, effective, isophotal, with or without atmosphere): e.g. R: SVO pivot 6498 A vs SVO mean 6586 A vs BM12 lambda_p 6515 A; SDSS g: SVO pivot 4702.5 A vs F96 lambda_eff 4825 A vs D10 lambda_eff 4627 A. Irrelevant at the precision of lambda/D, but the menu uses one definition (pivot) to stay consistent.
- **Band widths.** FWHM depends on the curve (BM12 revised photonic vs Bessell 1990), on energy vs photon normalisation (2MASS J: 1830 vs 2149 A), and on the definition (F96 lists the FWHM of an effective Gaussian, not of the curve; C03 'Bandwidth'; T12 square-bandpass fit 918-1001 nm for y). MKO M': SVO FWHM 0.2416 um vs T02 50%-point specification 0.220 um.
- **y band.** PS1 y pivot 0.9628 um vs LSST/Rubin y 0.9730 um (SVO); FWHM 665 vs 930 A.
- **Moon.** NSSDC 1896" (at the NSSDC 'Distance from Earth (equator)' of 378,000 km) vs geocentric 1865" at the 384,400 km semimajor axis (DERIVED).
- **Moffat beta.** 4.765 (T01, Kolmogorov, unit-flux fit) vs 5 (T01's rounded 'turbulence prediction' in its own models) vs 2.5 (IRAF/DAOPHOT `moffat25`, and the low end of T01's 'typical' 2.5-4 for real images). A unit-peak fit to the same Kolmogorov profile gives ~6.2 (DERIVED).
- **Airy coefficient.** 1.029 (RBH12; DERIVED 1.02899) vs 1.028 (D15, truncated).

### Notes

- SVO computes lambda_mean, lambda_cen, FWHM and W_eff on the energy-counting response (photon curves multiplied by lambda, peak-normalised), but the pivot on the photon-counting response (energy curves divided by lambda). Verified by recomputation from every saved VOTable (DERIVED); the tooltip text on the SVO page says 'energy counter' for the pivot too, which the numbers contradict.
- SVO's VOTables for 2MASS report Cohen et al. (2003) 'Specified' isophotal wavelengths in the WavelengthRef/Mean/Eff fields (12350, 16620, 21590 A); the menu uses the HTML 'Calculated' pivot.
- SVO's V-band page header says 'Bessell B generic filter' (typo); the curve is V (matches Bessell 1990 Table 2 V column).
- The SVO HTML pages also list the other filters of the same family in an overview table; each quote above comes from the selected filter's 'Filter Description' block.
- IRAF: the word 'default' is T01's ("current packages of data reduction in IRAF suggest a default value of β = 2.5"). In the saved IRAF sources, beta = 2.5 is the fixed exponent of DAOPHOT's `moffat25` PSF option (daopars default function: `gauss`), and imexamine/psfmeasure fit beta (default INDEF).
- Moffat FWHM relation (T01 Eq. 1): FWHM = 2 alpha sqrt(2^(1/beta) - 1); alpha/FWHM = 1.2636 for beta = 4.765 and 0.8846 for beta = 2.5 (DERIVED).
