## Completeness summary

**UHRF** (AAT coude, R ~ 1e6; the highest resolving power of any optical astronomical spectrograph) is well documented for the geometry that matters to the explorer: the AAT feed (3.893 m, f/37.5, 1.41 arcsec/mm), the 35-slice confocal image slicer (30 um slices, 0.0424 arcsec each, a 20 mm output slit), a 300 mm fused-silica singlet lens used in double pass as collimator, a 31.6 /mm 204 x 408 mm echelle (blaze 64.95 deg measured, 2 deg in-plane beam separation), four cross-dispersing gratings (2400/1800/1200/1200 /mm, 110 mm) after the echelle, a fixed aspheric camera lens with two focal reducers (the 1.0M, 0.6M and 0.3M modes), the Thomson, Tektronix, MITLL and E2V CCDs, and a full theoretical spectral format table (Appendix A3 of the manual) that fixes the effective camera focal length (10.34 m) and the order separations. Measured resolving powers: 993 000 and 990 000 (He-Ne laser, 1992-93), 940 000 in the manual. **Gaps that matter**: no collimator focal length or beam diameter (EFFECTIVE values f_col 9212 mm and W 245.6 mm are fitted from the published projection factor and dispersion), no echelle gamma, no cross-disperser blaze angles, the cross-disperser needs an EFFECTIVE groove density (471 /mm for the real 1200 /mm V grating) because a secondary collimator compresses the beam, and no decommissioning date (last documented use April 2010; the AAT lists it under previous instruments). **Recommended**: the 1.0M mode with the slicer and the Tektronix 1024 x 1024 24 um CCD (1994-2000), with the 0.6M and 0.3M modes as Settings. **Deploy gate (collimator)**: a lens, not a mirror; no on-axis obscuration issue.

## Conflicts between sources

- **Coude focal ratio.** D95 says f/37.5; Barlow et al. (B95, p. 335) say f/31.5 with a 710 um/arcsec scale; WD85 and the manual call the coude beam f/36. Adopted f/37.5 (1.41 arcsec/mm, 0.708 mm/arcsec; the f/31.5 reading would give 0.594 mm/arcsec, not 710 um). 1.40 arcsec/mm in the manual and cookbook corresponds to f/37.7.
- **Slicer entrance aperture.** D95: the CIS-35 slices an area of 1.0 x 1.5 arcsec2; the manual and the AAT page: 1.5 x 1.5 arcsec. Adopted 1.5 x 1.5 (1.5/35 = 0.0429 arcsec = the 30 um slice).
- **Slices used.** About 26 (manual, 2000), about 24 (cookbook, 1999), 18 (B95, 1994 masked), 14 (D95 Fig. 3), 27 in the 1998 MITLL2 test (CBT). The mask was changed over the years; not a model parameter.
- **Slicer entrance size in the cookbook.** The cookbook says the entrance is "a pin-hole of size 20 mm (about 1.5 arcsec)"; 20 mm is the output slit length, 1.5 arcsec = 1.07 mm. Treated as a misprint.
- **Resolving power of the 1.0M mode.** 993 000 and 990 000 (D95, laser, Thomson), 940 000 (manual, B95 Table 1, S13 MITLL3), 910 000 (C97, Tek), 845 000 (S13, EEV2), 700 000 (CN) and 800 000 (CH, CH+) from indirect Th-Ar widths (C94a, which says "for reasons still being investigated" this indirect value is lower). The instrument's published nominal is 1e6; recommended target 9.4e5 (the manual's "actually 940000").
- **Theoretical versus measured dispersion.** Table A3 (theory): 0.1480 A/mm at 6328 A; measured 0.1342 A/mm (10% lower). The camera focal length that fits the theory is 10.34 m, the measured 11.4 m. Recommended: the theory (the manual's tables and the projection factors come from the same ray trace).
- **Collimated beam diameter.** 255 mm (25% over the 204 mm width, INFERRED), 245.6 mm (from the slit magnification and the camera fit, DERIVED), 166 mm (just filling the echelle). Recommended 245.6 mm.
- **Slit-mode width for R = 1e6.** The manual says a 40 um slit = 0.0588 arcsec gives 1e6 (0.68 mm/arcsec); the plate scale of 0.7078-0.712 mm/arcsec gives 0.0562-0.0565 arcsec. Slit mode only.
