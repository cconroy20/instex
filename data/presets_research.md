# Spectrograph presets: sourced optical parameters

Compiled 2026-10-16 for the instex Spectrograph Explorer. Every number below comes from a document saved unmodified under `rawdata/presets/<instrument>/`; the source key, location and a short quote accompany each value. **DERIVED** marks arithmetic done here from sourced values (the arithmetic is shown). Quotes come from pdftotext/HTML text with glyphs normalised (degree signs, double primes); for the two ADS scans (Oke et al. 1995; Oke & Gunn 1982) the quotes come from the OCR text layer, with key values checked against the page images.

## Completeness summary

**HIRES**: the most complete. The 1994 user manual (UCO/Lick TR 67) gives collimator and camera focal lengths, beam, echelle (52.68 gr/mm, 70.5 deg, 2theta = 10 deg in-plane), the red cross-disperser (250 gr/mm, as-made 4.3 deg blaze, 40 deg turn) and per-order dispersion. Keck pages give the 2004 three-chip MIT-LL mosaic and a calculated-plus-measured R table per decker. Missing: the as-built UV cross-disperser and a numeric gamma. **MIKE**: good optics from the 2002 author manuscript of the SPIE paper (500/550 mm double-pass camera-collimators, 150 mm pupil, R2 52.6 gr/mm / R2.6 52.7 gr/mm echelles, PBM2 47 deg and 2 x 38 deg fused-silica prisms). The LCO manual and Bernstein+2015 give the as-used performance and differ from the manuscript on R, f/#, scale and the blue echelle (R2.4 vs R2.6). Quasi-Littrow angles were NOT FOUND. **HARPS**: complete for the echelle, collimator, camera, detector and order format (User Manual 2.4). The grism apex angle and the in-plane angle were NOT FOUND; the four ESO sources disagree on R (115k vs 120k) and on sampling (3.2-4.1 px). **Long slit (chosen: Keck/LRIS red)**: collimator 2000 mm, camera 305 mm f/1.56, beam 141 mm, 44 deg camera-collimator angle, f/15 (f/13.7), and the Keck grating table (A/pix, coverage, FWHM for a 1" slit). Blaze angles come only as blaze wavelengths. **DESI**: all three arms. As-built VPH line densities and incidence angles (2022 overview), FDR camera focal lengths (f/1.7, ~213 mm) and collimator (f/3.57, 126 mm pupil), detectors, fibre and resolution ranges. The native A/pixel is DERIVED. **Glass**: Malitson fused silica (publisher abstract), SCHOTT N-BK7, N-FK5 and FK5HTi (datasheets), OHARA PBM 2Y and PBM 2R (datasheets) plus the OHARA formula definition. Plain "PBM2" and classic "FK5" datasheets were NOT FOUND.

Long-slit choice: LRIS has a refereed instrument paper (O95) giving collimator focal length, camera focal length and f/#, collimated-beam diameter, the camera-collimator angle, telescope f/# and plate scale, detector pixel and on-sky scale, grating dispersions and a measured narrow-slit image width; Keck's current web pages add a full red-grating table (grooves/blaze wavelength, A/pix, coverage, FWHM for a 1" slit) and current detector specs. Palomar DBSP (OG82 + Palomar spec page) was the runner-up: comparable optics data but dispersions only in A/mm, no published resolution, and the red camera has since been replaced (152 mm in 1982 vs 12 in now).

## Keck I / HIRES (echelle, grating cross-disperser)

**Recommended preset:** HIRESr (red cross-disperser, 250 gr/mm, 1st order), decker C2 (0.861" x 14"), post-2004 3-chip MIT-LL mosaic; validate against measured R = 50,000 (Keck slit table, red XD). For a pre-2004 preset use the Tektronix 2048^2/24 um detector and R = 45,000 (manual Table 3, 0.861" slit).

## Glass dispersion formulas

### Formula forms

- **Sellmeier_SCHOTT**: `n^2(lambda) - 1 = B1 lambda^2/(lambda^2 - C1) + B2 lambda^2/(lambda^2 - C2) + B3 lambda^2/(lambda^2 - C3)`; lambda in um (C in um^2). Source: SCOL, Explanations page ("Constants of the dispersion formula"): "When calculating the refractive index using the Sellmeier coefficients from the SCHOTT data sheets, the wavelength λ needs to be entered in units of µm."
- **Sellmeier_OHARA**: `n^2 - 1 = A1 lambda^2/(lambda^2 - B1) + A2 lambda^2/(lambda^2 - B2) + A3 lambda^2/(lambda^2 - B3)`; lambda in um (B in um^2). Source: OHWEB, Sec. 2.3 Dispersion Formula: "As a practical dispersion formula, we have adopted the use of the Sellmeier formula ... When calculating a respective refractive index, please bear in mind that each wavelength is expressed in µm units."
- **Malitson_1965**: `n^2 - 1 = 0.6961663 lambda^2/(lambda^2 - 0.0684043^2) + 0.4079426 lambda^2/(lambda^2 - 0.1162414^2) + 0.8974794 lambda^2/(lambda^2 - 9.896161^2)`; lambda in um (constants in the denominators are wavelengths in um, squared). Source: MAL65, publisher abstract: "The dispersion equation n2-1=0.6961663λ2λ2-(0.0684043)2+0.4079426λ2λ2-(0.1162414)2+0.8974794λ2λ2-(9.896161)2.where λ is expressed in microns was found to yield an absolute residual of 10.5×10−6."

### Notes

- Consistency check (DERIVED): the Littrow dispersion computed from the sourced echelle constants and the DERIVED effective camera focal lengths (270/280/177 mm) reproduces the dispersion implied by XUM24 Table 13 to 4% (UVB), 1.4% (VIS) and 1.1% (NIR) -- supporting the beam (100/100/85 mm) + camera f/# derivation.
- Echelle geometry (interpretation): 'off-blaze angle' = in-plane offset theta = 0.0 deg in all arms (B08 Sec. 2 uses the same term: 'an Echelle grating with constant σE at off-blaze angle θ'); 'off-plane angle' = gamma (2.2 / 2.0 / 1.8 deg); N08 calls the NIR echelle 'near-Littrow'.
- UVB and VIS share an ESO FIERA controller; UVB/VIS have ADCs, NIR does not (V11 Sec. 2.2.7).
- M120 (2005) is a PDR-era design: NIR 1.03-2.48 um 'in 15 orders', four-lens camera; D06 (2006 FDR) introduced the slower F/2.1 NIR camera.

## Glass dispersion formulas, round 2 (X-Shooter prisms)

### Formula forms

- **Sellmeier_SCHOTT**: `n^2(lambda) - 1 = B1 lambda^2/(lambda^2 - C1) + B2 lambda^2/(lambda^2 - C2) + B3 lambda^2/(lambda^2 - C3)`; um (C in um^2); valid 'from the near UV to 2.3 um'. Source: SCOL, Explanations page: "the refractive indices for any wavelength within the range from the near UV to 2.3 µm can be calculated with the help of the constants B1, B2, B3, and C1,C2,C3. When calculating the refractive index using the Sellmeier coefficients from the SCHOTT data sheets, the wavelength λ needs to be entered in units of µm."
- **Sellmeier_Heraeus**: `n^2 - 1 = B1 lambda^2/(lambda^2 - C1) + B2 lambda^2/(lambda^2 - C2) + B3 lambda^2/(lambda^2 - C3)`; um, at 20 C (C in um^2). Source: HQGO, p. 3 'Refraction index dispersion / Dispersion constants (Sellmeier)': "n2 - 1 = B1 λ 2 /(λ 2 - C1) + B2 λ 2 /(λ 2 - C2 ) + B3 λ 2 /(λ 2 -C3 ) ... Wavelength λ in μm at 20°C"
- **Sellmeier_CHARMS_T**: `n^2(lambda,T) - 1 = sum_{i=1..3} S_i(T) lambda^2/(lambda^2 - lambda_i(T)^2), with S_i(T) = sum_{j=0..4} S_ij T^j and lambda_i(T) = sum_{j=0..4} lambda_ij T^j`; lambda and lambda_i in um, T in K; ABSOLUTE index (in vacuum). Source: LFK05, Sec. 3 (worked BaF2 example at 1.0 um, 77 K): "Si (T) = ∑ Sij ⋅ T j ... λ i (T) = ∑ λ ij ⋅ T j"
- **Malitson_1965**: `n^2 - 1 = 0.6961663 lambda^2/(lambda^2 - 0.0684043^2) + 0.4079426 lambda^2/(lambda^2 - 0.1162414^2) + 0.8974794 lambda^2/(lambda^2 - 9.896161^2)`; um; 20 C; 0.21-3.71 um. Source: MAL65, publisher abstract (round 1): "where λ is expressed in microns was found to yield an absolute residual of 10.5×10−6."

### Notes

- Status: MMTOH states 'Hectospec and Hectochelle were permanently decommissioned in summer 2026' -- the preset describes a retired instrument.
- Sign convention for the app: with the camera on the far side of the collimator by 35 deg, use alpha = 22.83 deg and beta_std = -12.17 deg in sin a + sin b = m g lambda (270 @ H-alpha). The grating rotates (HOM07 Sec. 4.5 'move the grating to the removal angle (120 degrees)') to set lambda_c.
- Fibre slit: the fibre ends point at the local normal of the spherical collimator (radius of curvature of the fibre-end arc 54.138 in, HHM04 Sec. 3.3.3), so the collimator works on-axis for every fibre; the spatial magnification is 1/3.45 = 0.290.
- Pixel scale along the slit (DERIVED): 13.5 um x 3.45 = 46.6 um at the fibre = 0.28" per pixel (166.7 um/arcsec); not physically meaningful for fibres but useful for the app's spatial axis.

### Conflicts between sources

- Camera focal length: 25.3 in = 642.6 mm (HCOG, for the grating equation) vs 0.49 x 1368.55 = 670.6 mm (DERIVED from S11's 0.49 demagnification and collimator radius) vs mirror R/2 = 675.5 mm. The S11 'nominal R = 32,000' from a 69 um fibre image fits 670.6 mm (32,000) better than 642.6 mm (30,700) (DERIVED).
- Resolution: 38,000 typical (S11) vs 'nominal' 32,000 (S11, fibre diameter) vs ~34,000 (HCOG) vs 32,000-40,000 and ~32,000 (MMTOH) vs ~32,000 (CFAHC).
- Sampling: '5 pixels along dispersion' (S11 text) vs '6 (disp.) x 10 (spat.)' (S11 Table 1) vs measured FWHM 3.4-4.2 px (S11 Sec. 3.4).
- Grating size: two 302 x 410 mm clear apertures (S11) vs 'a pair of 300 mm x 400 mm' (HCOG); mosaic 320 x 850 mm (S11 Sec. 4).
- Filters: S11 Table 5 lists OB26 at 6632 A / 241 A and OB24 at 6707/282 A; HCOG (2023) lists those as out-of-spec, adds CJ26 (626 nm), new OB24 (677.5 nm) and Cu28 (581.5 nm). The DERIVED order-26 centre is 6258 A, so the 6632 A 'OB26' sits in order 25 (HCOG: 'Hα at edge, but order overlap occurs').
- MMTOH says 11 filters ('there are 11 order-separting filters available'); HCOG Table 4 has 14 rows including superseded filters.
- Plate scale: 170 um/arcsec (S11) vs 250 um = 1.5" -> 166.7 um/arcsec (Hectospec documents; DERIVED).

### Notes

- Status: MMTOH states both Hecto instruments 'were permanently decommissioned in summer 2026'.
- Order bookkeeping for the app: the number in each filter name is the echelle order (DERIVED check: m lambda = 162,702 A gives 5248 A for order 31 vs RV31 CWL 5230 A; 6508 A for order 25 vs OB25 6563 A; 3968 A for order 41 vs Ca41 3950 A).
- There is no cross-disperser: 240 single-order spectra are stacked along the slit (spatial) direction on two CCDs. For the app, model a one-order echelle with an ideal order-sorting bandpass (filter FWHM ~ FSR).
- Fibre-image R boost: S11 states that projecting the circular fibre image onto the dispersion axis 'boosts this resolution by a factor of 1.46' relative to R computed from the image diameter -- the same ~0.68 FWHM/diameter ratio that G-CLEF's published R values imply (see G-CLEF Notes). Useful for the app's fibre LSF model.

## GMT / G-CLEF (fiber-fed, vacuum, asymmetric-white-pupil R4 echelle; dichroic to blue/red arms with VPH cross-dispersers) -- design values, NOT as-built

**Recommended preset:** GMT design of 2016-2018 (post-PDR / pre-CDR: GC16D, GC16O, GC18O, GC18D), RED arm (540-950 nm), MR mode: single circular 300 um fibre, 0.8" (800 um at the f/8 slit), R = 35,000 -- no slicing, so the app models it directly with D = 25.4 m, f/8. High-resolution variant: PRV/NS-PRV mode (R = 105,000-108,000) = 7 pupil slices; map to the app as ONE 8.4 m sub-aperture with a 0.8" fibre (or 25.4 m with a 0.265" slit) -- see Notes. Validation caveat: the published R values imply LSF FWHM ~0.67 x the geometric fibre-image width (DERIVED). Both arms are documented; the blue arm needs a VPH grism with two fused-silica prisms (32.42 deg, 15.87 deg), whose orientation is not published.

### Notes

- Pupil slicing and the app (DERIVED reasoning): in PRV/NS-PRV each of the 7 fibres sees the whole 0.8" star image through ONE 8.4 m segment (100 um at f/3 on 8.4 m = 0.82"), and each fibre's f/8 output fills the full 300 mm beam. The spectrum of each slice is therefore that of an 8.4 m telescope with a 0.8" fibre; the 7 slices are stacked along the slit and add signal. Model it as D = 8.4 m with a 0.8" fibre (or D = 25.4 m with a 0.265" fibre = GC14D's '0.26" (7x)'); multiply collected flux by 7 (pupil area of all segments).
- R validation caveat (DERIVED): with f_cam,eff = 675 mm and tan(theta_B) = 4.0, a Littrow R computed with LSF FWHM = geometric fibre-image width gives 72,000 (PRV, 75 um), 24,000 (MR, 225 um), 16,000 (HT, 337.5 um) -- vs published 108k/35k/19k. The published PRV and MR values imply FWHM = 0.67-0.69 x the image diameter (GC14D Table 5: FWHM 3.65 x 15 um = 54.8 um vs an 83 um geometric image, ratio 0.66). A uniformly lit disk projected on the dispersion axis has FWHM = 0.866 x D (R = 83k PRV). Hectochelle's paper quotes a 1.46 'boost' for the same effect.
- White pupil mapping: the app's collimator = M1 (2400 mm, 300 mm beam, f/8); camera = 675 mm effective on the 300 mm beam (physical 450 mm on the 200 mm beam after the 1.5x pupil transfer). The cross-disperser sits in the 200 mm beam, so its angular dispersion is multiplied by 450 mm (not 675 mm) at the detector.
- Design history: 2012 concept (GC12) -> 2014 PDR (GC14D/GC14O; PDR held April 2015 per GC16O) -> 2016 post-PDR re-optimisation (GC16D/GC16O) -> 2018 pre-CDR (GC18D/GC18O) -> 2023 G@M plan (GCM23) -> 2024-2025 assembly/testing (GC24, GC25). Values in the preset are 2016-2018.
- Glass: not needed for the recommended red-arm preset (pure VPH grating). A blue-arm preset would need fused silica for the two grism prisms (Malitson 1965 coefficients are in round 1, key MAL65) plus the unpublished prism orientation.
