## Completeness summary

**Primary**: 6.502 m specified and clear (6512 mm overall), a concave paraboloid with a 16256 mm radius of curvature (f = 8128 mm, f/1.25), Ohara E6 borosilicate honeycomb spin-cast at the Steward Observatory Mirror Lab. Central hole 0.898 m (MIR25, and an ADS snippet of the 2001 PISCES paper; no MMTO document gives the hole). No official collecting area (DERIVED 29.7-31.1 m^2 depending on the focus).

**Foci**: a classical Cassegrain with three interchangeable convex hyperboloidal secondaries; each focus lies behind the primary on the one instrument rotator, after 2 reflections (TR35 prescriptions, ICD03 summary table). The 1.7 m Zerodur f/5 secondary (1688 mm clear aperture, 1715 mm overall) serves three configurations: the wide-field corrector in the spectroscopic mode (f/5.29, 0.167 mm/arcsec, 1 deg field on a curved telecentric surface, with ADC: Binospec; Hectospec and Hectochelle until 2026), in the imaging mode (f/5.36, 0.169 mm/arcsec, 0.5 deg flat: Megacam), and bare (f/5.16 quoted, f/5.146 paraxial, 0.162 mm/arcsec: MMIRS, with the instrument's f/5.2 corrector over 14', INFERRED to use the bare focus). The 1.02 m Hextek borosilicate f/9 secondary gives f/9.00, 0.284 mm/arcsec, 12' unvignetted, 1.778 m behind the vertex (Blue Channel, SPOL). The f/15 adaptive secondary (a 640 mm Zerodur shell 1.8-1.9 mm thick on 336 actuators, refurbished for MAPS) gives f/15, 0.463 mm/arcsec, a 5-6' field, 2.286 m behind the vertex; undersized from an f/14.6 parent, the shell is the aperture stop, so the f/15 pupil is 6.37 m (MIR25; DERIVED three ways from TR35).

**Pupil**: the obstruction changes with the secondary: the 1.715 m f/5 mirror (eps 0.264, a lower bound, since the cone and ring baffles of the f/5 are unmeasured here), the 1.022 m f/9 mirror (0.157), and at f/15 the primary's 0.9 m hole (0.141 of 6.37 m). Spider: four radial vanes, 0.12 m wide, at 45 deg in the observatory's own wavefront-sensor code (mmtwfs); MIR25 models ~0.1 m and a PISCES snippet says 50 mm. Taken to be the same at all foci (INFERRED: the f/15 hub stays attached for all Cassegrain modes, W97). Gaps: the vane orientation relative to the elevation axis, the hole and spider in an MMTO document, the f/5 baffle and f/9 hub diameters, an official collecting area.

## Drawing (web/imager.js designs)

The drawing supports prime, cass, greg (each with an optional flat Nasmyth tertiary), korsch, pb, schmidt and refr. The MMT is a classical Cassegrain at all five foci, so the five use "cass": imager.js puts a convex secondary inside the prime focus at s = (m f1 - b)/(m + 1), with m = F/F1 and b = back x D. With f1 = 8.128 m and the TR35 back focal distances this reproduces the as-built secondary heights for the bare foci: 6178.0 mm at bare f/5 (TR35 6178.00), 6919.9 mm at f/9 (6919.952) and 7311.6 mm at f/15 on the 6.37 m pupil (7307.470). Schematic in the drawing:

- The f/5 wide-field corrector is not drawn (imager.js draws a corrector lens only at a prime focus). The corrector's power lengthens the focal length from 33.46 m (bare) to 34.45 m (spectroscopic) and 34.86 m (imaging), so at the two corrected foci the drawing puts the secondary at 6233.6 and 6248.4 mm, where the telescope has 6184.96 and 6184.42 mm; the three lenses (the first 0.83 m across, 29 mm behind the vertex), the ADC prisms and the field flattener are absent. A faithful drawing needs a Cassegrain corrector in imager.js and imgapp.js: a thin lens just behind the vertex that turns f/5.15 into f/5.30 (spectroscopic) or f/5.36 (imaging).
- The obstruction is drawn as eps D (the f/5 and f/9 mirrors' overall diameters; the f/15 hole); the f/5 cone baffle on M2, the rings on guy wires between M2 and M1, and the f/9 top box are not drawn.
- At f/15 the drawn primary is the 6.37 m pupil (the outer 66 mm annulus of the 6.502 m mirror, unseen by the undersized secondary, is left out).
- The primary's hole in the 2D drawing is sized to pass the returning beam; the 3D view cuts the sourced 0.898 m.
- The vanes' orientation (45 deg) is the mmtwfs model frame; the MMT's orientation relative to the elevation axis is NOT FOUND.

## Notes

- All foci are Cassegrain foci on the one instrument rotator behind the primary (ICD03); the MMT has no Nasmyth or prime focus. The foci differ only by the secondary (and, at f/5, the corrector): 2 reflections each.
- The entrance pupil is the primary (6.502 m) at f/5 and f/9; at f/15 the undersized adaptive secondary is the stop, so the pupil shrinks to 6.37 m and the primary's 0.9 m hole becomes the obstruction (the 0.64 m shell lies in the hole's shadow).
- The spider is taken to be the same for all three secondaries (INFERRED: W97 says the f/15 hub stays attached for all Cassegrain modes, and MWC uses one telescope-level spider for the f/5 and f/9 wavefront sensors).
- Status (2026): the f/5 secondary is on the telescope ~30% of the time (MMTC); Binospec and MMIRS are the available f/5 instruments; Hectospec and Hectochelle were decommissioned in summer 2026 (MMTOH); Blue Channel and SPOL are available at f/9 (the f/9 was expected to be phased out in 2003, ICD03); the f/15 with MAPS is shared risk (MMTAO, MMTI).
- TR35 Fig. 1 and the ICD03 flange distances agree: 1466.85 mm + 12.25 in = 1778.0 mm (f/9) and + 32.25 in = 2286.0 mm (f/15).
- Pupil demagnification (TR35): f/5 4.13, f/9 6.69, f/15 9.93; the f/15 beam would be 655 mm at the secondary, more than the 642 mm mirror: hence the stop.
- Quotes embedded in notes and the summary (C13 Fig. 3 caption, ICD03 Introduction, the MMTI table rows, the MMTTR index line, the MWC docstring, the PIS01 abstract, W97 Fig. 2 heights, MMTT "10-ton", FF94 "approximately 300 kg", M15 corrector, MMIRS12 Lyot stop) are in the quote check.
