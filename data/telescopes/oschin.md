The Oschin is a Schmidt: the aperture stop is the corrector plate at the spherical mirror's center of curvature, and the focus lies halfway between, inside the tube, where the plateholder or camera faces the mirror and blocks the incoming beam. The mirror, the stop and the focal length have not changed since 1948; the corrector (a singlet, then an achromatic doublet, then the doublet with the ZTF trim plate), the detector at the focus and the vanes that hold the detector (four flat vanes, then three raked vanes) have.

## Completeness summary

**Stop and mirror**: The stop is the corrector at the mirror's center of curvature, 6.124 m in front of the mirror (D20): 49.5 in = 1.2573 m clear in the photographic era (H52, ATL54, R91) and 1244.6 mm as the ZTF entrance pupil (D20 Table 1; D20 gives 1244 mm for the original singlet too: Conflicts). The mirror is a spherical Pyrex mirror 72 in = 1.8288 m across, radius of curvature 241.16 in = 6125.5 mm (H52; 6124 mm in D20), so f = R/2 = 3.06 m (f/1.67 on the mirror's own diameter; DERIVED), with a central hole for the radial bearing (size NOT FOUND). One reflection.

**Correctors**: POSS-I (1949-1958): the original singlet of 3/8-in plate glass figured on both sides (53 in overall, 50 in usable), corrected for 4358 A (H52, R91; 4861 A in ATL54). POSS-II (1985-2000): the Grubb Parsons achromatic doublet, Schott LLF6 + O'Hara BK7W, 50 in, installed in 1985 (R91, S14; 1982 in D20 and R17). ZTF (2017-): the same doublet with an air-spaced fused-silica trim plate at the stop, an 'air-spaced triplet' (D20).

**Focal length and field**: Plates: f/2.44 and 67.187"/mm (H52; 67.19 in R91), f = 3.070 m (DERIVED), F = 2.4417 on 1.2573 m; 14-in plates of 6.6 x 6.6 deg, unvignetted to 5.4 deg (R91, ATL54; H52's 28.6 cm = 5.34 deg, which the mirror walk-off reproduces). ZTF: f/2.46 on 1244.6 mm (3.062 m) and 1.01" per 15 um pixel = 67.33"/mm (D20, B19); the mosaic spans 7.32 x 7.50 deg, and the mosaic's corners lose up to 30% to walk-off (unvignetted 5.45 deg, DERIVED). The focal surface is a sphere of radius = f (120.9 in, R91; 3062 mm, D20), concave toward the mirror: the plates were bent onto mandrels; ZTF's cryostat window flattens the curvature to a 7000 mm radius, and field flatteners over the CCDs on a faceted cold plate remove the rest.

**Pupil**: Plates: the plateholder at the center of the tube (size NOT FOUND; the 14-in plate as a lower bound, 10.2% of the pupil, area-equivalent eps 0.319) on four flat vanes in a '+' along north-south and east-west (D20, D16, ATL54; the four diffraction spikes on the POSS-I and POSS-II plates, DSS1V and DSS2V; the PALI photograph); vane width NOT FOUND (20 mm INFERRED). ZTF: 22.4-22.5% of the pupil blocked on axis, 2.3% of that by three raked vanes (D20); the camera blocks 20.2%, drawn as the octagonal cryostat window (483 x 442 mm, D20) grown by a 19.6 mm border to that area (INFERRED shape); the vanes radial at 90, 210 and 330 deg (S14 Fig. 3; the six spikes in ZTFV) and 27.3 mm wide (DERIVED from the 2.3%).

**Gaps**: the plateholder's size; the plate-era vane width; the ZTF vane width and camera outline beyond the area fractions; whether the single ZTF vane points north or south; the correctors' prescriptions.

## Drawing (web/imager.js design "schmidt")

imager.js design "schmidt" places the stop (an ideal corrector of no power) at z = -2f, the spherical mirror at 0 and the focus at f, all from F and D: node runs of `IM.design` with the proposed entries give f = 3061.7 mm (ZTF) and 3069.9 mm (plates), the stop 6123.4 and 6139.9 mm in front of the mirror (6124 mm in D20, 6125.5 mm in H52) and the focus 3061.7 and 3069.9 mm from the mirror; one star, one point (spread 0). imgapp.js draws the mirror as large as the field's bundles at the mirror (x 1.02): 1864 mm (ZTF, field 327') and 1873 mm (plates, 324'), compared with the real 1828.8 mm, so the drawing shows the mirror larger than the stop for the reason the real one is. The prime-focus box (pfu) at the focus is max(drawEps D/2, 1.2 x the field's half-width) tall: 559 mm for ZTF (the real cryostat window is 483 x 442 mm), 401 mm for the plateholder.

What the drawing shows faithfully: the stop at the corrector, the oversized spherical mirror 2f behind the stop, the focus halfway and facing the mirror, the plateholder or camera in the incoming beam (the obstruction), the beams of the field's center and edge walking across the mirror, and the pupil with the block and vanes (2D panel and 3D view).

Schematic in the drawing, per focus. ZTF: the corrector is drawn as one thin plate of no power (really the cemented doublet plus the air-spaced trim plate, an aspheric triplet); the focal surface is drawn flat (really a sphere of radius 3062 mm, flattened to 7000 mm by the 32 mm meniscus window, then by a plano-convex field flattener over each CCD on a faceted cold plate: none drawn); the filter, the window and the hexapod are not drawn; the camera box is as tall as the area-equivalent circle of the published 20.2% (559 mm), and the pupil block is the window octagon with an INFERRED border; the three vanes are raked in the telescope and drawn in one plane at the camera (3D) or in the pupil; the field drawn is the 5.45 deg unvignetted circle (the mosaic is 7.32 x 7.50 deg). POSS-II: the corrector drawn as one plate (really the cemented achromatic doublet); the plates' spherical surface (R = 120.9 in) drawn flat; the filter in the plateholder not drawn; the plateholder box and the pupil block are the 14-in plate (INFERRED lower bound); vanes 20 mm (INFERRED). POSS-I: as POSS-II with the singlet plate (whose chromatic error the ideal drawing does not show); the two plate foci draw identically. The clam-shell (plates) and bi-parting (ZTF) shutters at the corrector are not drawn; the tube (6.65 m long, 2.1 m across) is not drawn.

No new drawing code is needed: the Schmidt branch exists in imager.js, imgapp.js and tel3d.js, and the blocks and per-focus pupils exist in tpupil.js and telapp.js. A curved focal surface (with the ZTF window and flatteners) would need a curved focus element in the Schmidt branch of imgapp.js and tel3d.js.

## Notes

- A Schmidt's stop is the corrector at the mirror's center of curvature (6.12 m in front of the mirror); the focus lies halfway, inside the tube, facing the mirror. The mirror (1.83 m) is larger than the stop (1.24-1.26 m) because each field angle uses a different part of the mirror: a beam at 2.7 deg walks 6.124 m x tan 2.7 deg = 0.29 m across the mirror (DERIVED), which sets the unvignetted field (5.34-5.45 deg, DERIVED; 5.4 deg published).
- The obstruction is the plateholder or camera in the incoming beam, between the corrector and the mirror; the returning beam converges onto the same object from the other side. The obstruction therefore shadows the pupil's center at each field angle, and the shadow grows off the axis (ZTF: 22.4% on axis, ~46% at the field's edge).
- The four plate-era vanes give four diffraction spikes along north-south and east-west (DSS1V, DSS2V); the three ZTF vanes give six (ZTFV; D20 Sec. 4.1). The ZTF spikes include an east-west pair.
- POSS-I and POSS-II used the same stop, mirror, focal length, plates and four-vane hub; the corrector (singlet vs achromatic doublet), the mandrels (reground for POSS-II) and the filter's place (in the POSS-II plateholder) changed. In the explorer the two foci differ only in their text.
- The CCD eras between the plates and ZTF are not proposed as foci: NEAT (from 2000-2001), QUEST (two years later) and the PTF camera (2008 until ZTF; the CFH12K mosaic behind a powered dewar window on the four-vane hub, B19 and D20; 19% obstruction, S14) (PAL).
- Reused documents (byte-identical copies): H52 and R91 from rawdata/imaging/poss/ (data/imaging/poss.md); D16, R17, B19 and D20 from rawdata/imaging/ztf/ (data/imaging/megacam_ztf_small.md). New: R40, ATL54, S14, PAL, PALI, PALO and the three Vega cutouts.
