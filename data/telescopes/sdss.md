## Completeness summary

**Primary**: 2.5 m nominal, f/2.25, nearly hyperbolic (R = 11249.86 mm, conic -1.285), Hextek borosilicate honeycomb, central hole 1.17 m (design). The as-built outer stop, just above the mirror, is 2.517 m (R1258.585 mm in the Zemax ray trace; "2.52 m" in G06), chosen to mask a turned edge: the explorer D. No official collecting area (DERIVED 3.553 m^2 on axis).

**Secondary and pupil**: convex, 1.08 m clear, vertex radius 7194 mm (the primary's curvature), conic -11.97, 3.645 m in front of the primary. On axis the pupil's inner edge is the secondary-baffle tip, 1.2846 m (measured 1285.7 mm): eps = 0.5103, 26.0% of the area. A conical baffle hung on eight wires between the mirrors adds 2.6% (28.6% in all on axis; 31.8% at the 1.5 deg field edge). The secondary hangs on tension rods ("vanes") along the diagonals of a square frame; their number and width are NOT FOUND, so the explorer draws no vanes.

**Foci**: one f/5 Cassegrain focus (2 reflections) with three corrector configurations, all with a 3 deg field. (1) SDSS-V (2021 onwards): a three-lens fused-silica corrector, focal surface R = 9199 mm, 0.776 m behind the primary's vertex, 500 robotic positioners (BOSS + APOGEE, 2" fibers), plate scale 217.7358 mm/deg (60.48 um/arcsec; F = 4.956 on 2.517 m). (2) SDSS-I to -IV spectroscopy on plug plates (to 2020): the Gascoigne corrector at the primary's vertex plus a thin aspheric spectroscopic corrector, the focus 0.757 m behind the vertex (F = 4.954, DERIVED from the design). (3) the SDSS imaging camera (early SDSS): the Gascoigne corrector plus the camera's thick corrector, EFL 12428.7 mm (F = 4.938). Gaps: the vanes, the place of the conical baffle's 2.6% in the pupil, an official collecting area and a plug-plate-era plate scale from an SDSS document.

## Drawing (web/imager.js designs)

Each configuration is design "cass" (a convex secondary inside the prime focus, the focus behind the primary). Given F, F1 = 2.2346 and back, the imager.js model puts the secondary 3.636 m (SDSS-V), 3.641 m (plug plates) and 3.636 m (imaging) in front of the primary, compared with the published 3.645, 3.644 and 3.646 m: the 5-10 mm differences are the weak power of the correctors, which the two-mirror model leaves out. The focal length, plate scale and focus position (0.76-0.78 m behind the vertex) are the telescope's. Schematic: the correctors are not drawn (imager.js draws a corrector only at a prime focus; here the Gascoigne plate or L1 sits at the primary's vertex and the final corrector 85-90 mm before the focus), nor the conical baffle (2.6%) or the secondary vanes. The drawn obstruction is the 1.285 m secondary-baffle shadow, larger than the 1.08 m secondary, so the pupil view shows the baffle, not the mirror. A faithful drawing of the correctors would need a Cassegrain corrector option in imager.js (thin lenses at given positions, as for the prime-focus corrector).

## Notes

- The three explorer foci share one pupil and one primary-secondary pair; they differ in the correctors, the secondary spacing (3646.14, 3644.46, 3645.17 mm) and the focal surface (0.759, 0.757, 0.776 m behind the primary's vertex).
- APOGEE (2011-2020 on plug plates, now on the robots) and BOSS share the f/5 focus; the APOGEE spectrograph preset is in data/round5/apogee.md (same G06 and W19 files).
- The obstruction grows off axis (31.8% at 1.5 deg) because the conical and primary baffles emerge from the secondary-baffle shadow beyond 0.5-0.7 deg; the explorer pupil is the on-axis one.
- The roll-off enclosure and the wind baffle sit outside the beam; the wind baffle's central disk lies in the secondary's shadow (APOA excludes it from the 28.6%).
