Each number comes from a document saved unmodified under `rawdata/telescopes/gemini/` (files reused from earlier rounds are byte-identical copies, marked in the source table); each value carries a source key, location and a short verbatim quote. **DERIVED** marks arithmetic done here (shown). Gemini North (Maunakea) and Gemini South (Cerro Pachon) are one design (G96), so they are entered as one telescope, as Keck I and II were. The telescope has a single focal station, the f/16 Cassegrain focus, where the Instrument Support Structure (ISS) carries the instruments on an up-looking port and on side-looking ports fed by a 45-deg science fold.

## Completeness summary

**Primary**: a thin ULE meniscus 8.1 m across (8090 mm on the drawing), 20 cm thick, with a 1.2 m central hole (1.18 m in two web pages); a concave hyperboloid, f = 14.402 m measured (f/1.8 on 8.1 m), conic -1.0038 (GN model). Coated with protected silver, as are the secondary and the science fold. Gemini publishes no collecting area: 47.57 m^2 for the pupil drawn here (DERIVED).

**Pupil**: the 1.023 m secondary is undersized and is the stop, so the entrance pupil is 7.9 m (Gemini's Zemax models; the paraxial image of the secondary is 7.92 m, DERIVED). Central obstruction in the infrared configuration: the 168 mm hole in the secondary, through which a periscope shows the instruments cold sky; the hole projects to 1.30 m, eps 0.164 (NICI page; 0.1642 DERIVED). With the deployable baffle extended for optical work (2.003 m), eps is 0.254 (DERIVED). Spider: four struts (GPI 2.0 paper), 10 mm wide (fact sheet; "1 cm", Sivaramakrishnan & Lloyd 2005). Their geometry is a FIGURE READ-OFF of a GPI pupil image: two pairs, each pair meeting ~0.21 m off the center, the two vanes of a pair at +/-46.5 deg from the line through the meeting points; the pattern's orientation is NOT FOUND.

**Secondary**: a convex Zerodur hyperboloid, 1.023 m (1022.66 mm at the virtual condition on the drawings), radius 4190.2 mm, conic -1.618 (GN model), 12.543 m above the primary (12.536 m on Gemini South), on a tip-tilt and chopping mount.

**Foci**: one Cassegrain focus, f = 128.12 m (f/16 quoted; f/16.22 on the 7.9 m pupil, DERIVED), 1.610 arcsec/mm (1.611 in the instrument documents), 4.0 m behind the primary's vertex at the up-looking port (10 arcmin field, two reflections). The science fold, 2.9 m behind the vertex (GS model), sends the beam to a side port 1.1 m off the axis (7 arcmin field, three reflections). Gaps: the spider's orientation and an official collecting area.

## Drawing (web/imager.js designs)

The drawing supports prime, cass, greg (each with an optional flat Nasmyth tertiary, which may sit behind the primary when a3 < 0, as for the GMT's folded port), korsch, pb, schmidt and refr. Gemini is a two-mirror Ritchey-Chretien Cassegrain, so "cass" draws all three foci. The cass model places the secondary from F, F1 = f1/D and the focus distance b: s = (m f1 - b)/(m + 1), m = F/F1. With D = 7.9 m, f1 = 14.402 m (F1 = 1.8230), F = 16.2177 and b = 4.0 m: m = 8.896, s = 12.542 m (GN model 12.543 m, GS 12.536 m) and f2 = -2.095 m (GN model R2/2 = -2.095 m). The drawing therefore reproduces the real mirror spacing.

- Up-looking port: the focus 4.0 m behind the vertex (back = 0.50633 D), two reflections.
- Side ports: a flat tertiary on the axis 2.9 m behind the vertex (a3 = -2.9, inside the ISS below the primary's hole) turns the beam by 90 deg to a focus 1.1 m off the axis (backN = 0.13924 D); b = backN D - a3 = 4.0 m, the same unfolded focus. Three reflections. The four side-looking faces are drawn as one side.
- Optical-baffle variant: the same as a side port with eps 0.2535 (per-focus eps, as for Keck's f/40).
- Schematic: the pupil is the on-axis 7.9 m circle; the secondary's sky periscope, the chimney baffle in the primary's hole and the ISS (acquisition and guiding unit, wavefront sensors) are not drawn; the focal surface is curved in reality (the models' name: "curved field"). The vanes are drawn in the pupil plane at the orientation chosen here. The proposed `coating: "silver"` needs a silver color in tel3d.js (only "gold" exists).

## Conflicts

- Primary diameter: 8.1 m (GTEL, GFP), 8090 mm (GBAF1) vs 8 m (G96 design; the Zemax semi-diameter 4000 mm) vs 8.2 m (GWTEL, a 2000 page).
- Central hole: 1.2 m (G96; GZMN obscuration radius 600 mm; GBAF2 aperture plate 1200 mm) vs 1.18 m (GFP, GWTEL).
- Secondary: 1.023 m (GFS), 1022.6554 mm (GBAF2, virtual condition) vs 1.0 m (GTEL) vs 1.02 m (G96); SiC (G96 design) vs Zerodur (GTEL, GFS).
- Central obstruction in the IR configuration: 0.164 (GNICI; 168/1023 = 0.1642 DERIVED) vs 0.1515 (GZMN inner clear radius 77.55 mm) vs 0.146 (P06: 1.18 m of 8.1 m) vs 0.181 (FIGURE READ-OFF, S14 Fig. 6, relative to the imaged pupil edge).
- Spider count: four struts (N22; four vanes in S14 Fig. 6) vs "two spider vanes" (P06, a lab simulator that modeled one vane).
- Plate scale: 1.610 arcsec/mm (GTEL) vs 1.611 (GSPEC, GICD) vs 1.60994 (DERIVED from 128.12 m) vs 1.61028 (GN model) and 1.61143 (GS model).
- Focal ratio: f/16 quoted (GTEL, G96, GALT) vs 16.2177 on the 7.9 m pupil (DERIVED; f/16.0 holds for an 8.0 m pupil).
- Distance from the science fold to a side-port focus: 1100 mm (GZMS) vs 1095 mm (DERIVED from GICD: 795 mm to the port land plus 300 mm).

## Notes

- One telescope for two: Gemini North and Gemini South share the design (G96: "identical in design"); the explorer entry stands for both, as the Keck entry does for Keck I and II. The prescriptions differ slightly (GZMN, GZMS); the pupil measurement is Gemini South's (GPI).
- Cassegrain only: all instruments mount on the ISS at the single f/16 focus, on the up-looking port or on a side port behind the 45-deg science fold (one more silver reflection). The adaptive optics systems sit on side ports and pass the f/16 beam on (GALT).
- The infrared design: the undersized secondary is the stop (7.9 m pupil), the secondary's 168 mm hole and a sky periscope show the infrared instruments cold sky in place of the primary's hole, the vanes are 10 mm thin, and the mirrors are silver. With the optical baffle extended (2.003 m) the obstruction grows to eps 0.254 and the periscope is shuttered.
- The explorer offers the optical-baffle configuration as a third focus (a side port with eps 0.2535); the instrument's wavelength range sets the baffle position (GTEL: two positions, IR and Optical).
- The NIRI preset (data/imaging/niri_hawki.md) uses D = 7.9 m, eps 0.164 and F = 16.22, the same telescope values.
