The Hale telescope has three foci: the prime focus in the observer's cage (f/3.3 bare, or behind a corrector), the Cassegrain f/16 behind the primary, and the coude f/30 on the polar axis; the f/16 beam can also go to the east arm of the yoke.

## Completeness summary

**Primary**: a ribbed Pyrex paraboloid 200 inches (5.08 m) across with a 40-inch (1.016 m) hole, f = 660 in = 16.764 m (f/3.3; 666 in in a 1948 paper), 24 inches thick at the edge, aluminized. Palomar quotes ~20 m^2 for the mirror; the pupil drawn here gives 17.43 m^2 (DERIVED).

**Pupil**: the full 5.08 m mirror (no undersized stop). Central obstruction: the 72-inch observer's cage (eps 0.360, B61); the measured pupil (hcipy, after the pupil image in Galicher et al. 2019) gives 1.86 m (eps 0.3661; 0.3687 by a fit to the same image, FIGURE READ-OFF). Spider: four radial vanes in a cross (G19: "horizontal and vertical lines"; hcipy) with short 0.12 m attachment boxes at their roots; width 0.25% of the aperture = 12.7 mm (Carlotti et al. 2014) vs 48 mm (hcipy, the blurred apparent width); an equivalent-width reading of the image gives 0.011-0.022 m.

**Secondaries**: all in the lower part of the cage: the Cassegrain convex hyperboloid, 41 inches, eccentricity ~1.52; two coude hyperboloids, 36 and 32 inches, eccentricity 1.25 (A48).

**Foci**: prime focus f/3.3 (12.304 arcsec/mm, < 2.6 arcmin of good images without a corrector); with the Wynne corrector (LFC, WaSP) 0.18 arcsec per 15 um pixel = 12.0 arcsec/mm (f/3.384, DERIVED), 24 arcmin; with Ross's 1950s correctors f/3.6 and f/4.7 over ~3 inches. Cassegrain f/16 (3200 in = 81.28 m, 2.538 arcsec/mm) ~5 feet behind the primary's front surface; f/15.7-15.8 in the DBSP documents. Coude f/30 (6000 in = 152.4 m, 1.353 arcsec/mm) down the polar axis, with one flat (3 reflections) or three (5). Gaps: corrector positions, the coude geometry, the spider width in an observatory document.

## Drawing (web/imager.js designs)

Prime focus ("prime"): the observer's cage drawn as the prime-focus unit (eps 0.36, the 72-inch cage). With a corrector that changes the focal length (Wynne: F/F1 = 1.0253; Ross: 1.0909) imager.js draws one thin lens d = 0.5 m before the primary's focus with the power that takes f/3.3 to the corrector's F (a weak negative lens, the "small telephoto effect" B61 describes); the lens's place and size are schematic (NOT FOUND). Without a corrector the drawing is the bare paraboloid.

- Cassegrain ("cass"): s = (m f1 - b)/(m + 1) with F1 = 3.3, m = 16/3.3 = 4.848 and b = 1.524 m gives s = 13.637 m (3.127 m inside the prime focus) and f2 = -3.939 m; the on-axis beam on the secondary is 0.948 m (37.3 in), inside the 41-inch mirror (A48). The drawing shows the secondary by itself; in the telescope the secondary hangs below the cage, inside the cage's shadow.
- Coude ("cass", unfolded): a coude train cannot be drawn. The focus is drawn as a Cassegrain-type focus with F = 30 and back = 2.694 D, which puts the secondary 13.746 m above the primary (3.018 m inside the prime focus, where the on-axis beam fills the 36-inch coude secondary) and the unfolded focus 13.69 m behind the primary. The flat on the 36-inch tube through the primary's hole, which sends the beam down the polar axis, is not drawn (the fieldNote says so); the flat's height and the secondary's place are NOT FOUND. Leaving the coude out is the alternative.
- Not drawn: the east-arm focus (the f/16 beam sent along the declination axis), the coude flats, the cage's structure, the tube.

## Conflicts

- Primary focal length: 660 in, f/3.3 (B61, PHT) vs 666 in (A48, 1948); the LFC cookbook's prime focus "f/3.33" matches 666 in.
- Central obstruction: 72-inch cage, eps 0.360 (B61) vs 1.86 m, eps 0.3661 (HCI) vs 0.3687 (FIGURE READ-OFF of G19 Fig. 3).
- Spider width: 12.7 mm (C14, 0.25%) vs 48 mm (HCI) vs 0.017 m equivalent width (FIGURE READ-OFF, 0.011-0.022 m); hcipy's value matches the blurred apparent width.
- Prime focus with the Wynne corrector: 0.18 arcsec/px (PWASP, PLFC; f/3.384) vs 0.175 at the field center (PLFCC; f/3.480) vs "f/3.33" (PLFCC) vs "f/3.3" (PWASP, PLFC). WaSP's field: 18.4 x 18.5 arcmin (PWASP) vs 24 arcmin across (PHT; 24 arcmin is the LFC's field diameter, PLFC).
- Cassegrain: f/16, 81.28 m, 2.538 arcsec/mm (B61, PHT) vs f/15.7 and 2.5 arcsec/mm (OG82, mutually inconsistent: 79.76 vs 82.51 m) vs 2.57 arcsec/mm at the DBSP slit (PDS; 80.26 m, f/15.80).
- Coude flats: one flat south of +43 deg and three north of +43 deg (B61, 1961) vs the auxiliary flats for objects north of 50 deg (A48, 1948).
- Central hole: 40 in (B61) vs the 39 1/2-inch core that cast the hole (A48).
- G19 places the stellar double coronagraph "at the primary focus" of the Hale; the coronagraph sits between PALM-3000 and PHARO, and the pupil image shows the cage's obscuration.

## Notes

- The cage at the prime focus is the obstruction at all foci: the cage's lower part holds the three hyperboloids (the f/16 Cassegrain and two f/30 coude secondaries), which are swung into the beam when needed (B61). The explorer uses eps 0.3661 (the measured pupil) by default and 0.360 (the 72-inch cage) at the prime foci.
- Prime focus: the bare paraboloid gives good images over less than half an inch (2.6 arcmin), so the cage instruments work behind correctors: Ross's lenses at f/3.6 and f/4.7 in the photographic era (B61), a Wynne corrector for LFC and WaSP since the 2000s (PLFCC; WaSP INFERRED).
- The coude train (one flat for southern objects, three for northern) cannot be drawn: the explorer draws the coude focus unfolded as a Cassegrain-type focus, with the secondary's place INFERRED from the 36-inch coude secondary; the fieldNote says so. Leaving the coude out is the alternative.
- The 4-Shooter preset (data/imaging/fourshooter.md) used D 5.08, eps 0.36 and F 16; this file adds the measured pupil (eps 0.3661) and the vanes.
- The pupil frame is G19's image frame (vanes horizontal and vertical, as hcipy draws the vanes); the cross in the image is tilted ~2 deg, within the reading.
