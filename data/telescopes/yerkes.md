## Completeness summary

**Objective**: Alvan Clark & Sons air-spaced doublet (crown in front, flint behind; discs by Mantois, Paris; glass types NOT FOUND), clear aperture 40 in = 1.016 m; f = 19.354 m at 50 F (Barnard 1917; 1936 cm in 1899, 19.36 m in 1910, "very nearly 62 feet" in 1897) -> f/19.05 DERIVED; focal length shrinks 17.5 mm per 45 C cooling. Tube 60 ft (64 ft in 1893). First observations 1897 May 21.

**Focus**: one (refractor) focus at the eye end, 0 reflections, 4 refracting surfaces; plate scale 1" = 0.0937 mm (10.67"/mm; 10.6"/mm in 1910); photographic field used: 20 x 25 cm plates = 35' x 45'; unvignetted field NOT FOUND.

**Chromatic aberration (the pedagogical point)**: Fox (1908) Table IV gives the colour curve of the objective alone: best focus near 5700-5760 A; the focus moves out by 12.6 mm at H-beta, 51 mm at H-gamma, 89 mm at H-delta, 119 mm at 3969 A and 16 mm at 6800 A, i.e. blur circles of 7" (H-beta) to 67" (3969 A) at the visual focus (DERIVED). Photography used yellow screens + isochromatic plates (Ritchey 1900) or plates insensitive to 4800-5200 A (Schlesinger 1910: only 5200-5700 A in focus; 4500 A spread over ~2 mm). Hale & Ellerman (1899) published the same curve at -18 C as a figure.

**Pupil**: unobstructed circle, D = 1.016 m, eps = 0, no vanes (no obstruction described in any source; INFERRED from the refractor design).

## Conflicts

- Focal length: 19354 mm at 50 F (B17, micrometer; used by FOX) vs 1936 cm (HE99, star trails) vs 19.36 m (S10) vs "very nearly 62 feet" = 18.90 m (H97). Lens-to-focus distances 18966 mm (crown surface) and 18793 mm (lens-space centre) are not focal lengths (B17).
- Lens separation: 8 3/8 in (H97, page image) vs 8.6 in = 218 mm (B17) vs "about seven inches" (K96, 1895 test, before the lenses were re-positioned).
- Tube length: 60 ft (H97, 1897) vs 64 ft (P93, 1893).
- Plate scale: 1" = 0.0937 mm -> 10.67"/mm (B17) vs 10.6"/mm (S10) vs 10.657"/mm DERIVED from 19354 mm.
- Clear aperture: 40 in = 1016 mm (DERIVED) vs "102 cm" (HE99, rounded).

## Notes

- Pupil for the explorer: a filled circle of D = 1.016 m (eps = 0, no vanes): the diffraction pattern is the pure Airy pattern. Pedagogically the dominant aberration is chromatic: the colour curve (yerkes.color_curve) moves the focus by ~13 mm at H-beta and ~89 mm at H-delta relative to the best (5700 A) focus, i.e. geometric blur circles of ~7" and ~50" -- far larger than the 0.14" Airy radius at 5500 A (DERIVED 1.22 x 550e-9 / 1.016 rad).
- Reflections: none (refractor); 4 glass-air surfaces. F = 19.354/1.016 = f/19.05 (DERIVED).
- The objective was accepted in October 1895 after Keeler's test (K96), shipped to Williams Bay and first used on 1897 May 21 (HC97); Fox (1908) used the Hartmann test (T < 0.5).
- OCR artefacts kept verbatim in quotes: "io''6" = 10".6 (S10); "d=" = plus-minus (HE99); "—i8°C." = -18 C, "\ 5000"/"X6500"/"X4500" = lambda (HE99); "May ii" = May 11 (H97); "oí" = of (P93); "putign TI" = computing T I, "^" = r (FOX); "thé", "colorrcurve.", "40-Jnch" (R00); Fox Table IV OCR "457-88" = 457.88, "§66.37" = 566.37.
