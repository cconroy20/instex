# Audit S10: Keck I / ZShooter presets (zshb, zshg, zshr, zshyj, zshh, zshk)

Auditor's scope: web/presets.js lines 322–352 (block comment, the six-row table, the shared note, state and pub), data/round6/zshooter.md and its JSON twin, and the saved sources under rawdata/presets/zshooter/. The physics was rerun with web/physics.js `design()` in node for the six presets and for variants (scripts run1.js–run4.js in the scratchpad folder audit/zsh/). Headless screenshots of the B and K settings were taken on a local server (port 8847, stopped afterward): audit/zsh/zshb.png and audit/zsh/zshk.png. No project file was edited.

The live website pages (technical.html, zspec_optical.html, optical_design.html, performance.html) still carry HTTP Last-Modified 2026-10-16, the date of the saved copies, so the saved text is current. The SPIE 2026 instrument paper (Bailey et al., Proc. SPIE 14149-77) is still not obtainable: a web search found no arXiv or open copy, and the Crossref record has no abstract.

ZShooter is a design-stage instrument, so no measured value exists for any quantity. The preset state agrees with the published first-order prescription. The problems found are in the note text, in source attribution, in the circularity of three scored lines, and in two geometric assumptions (the sign and size of γ, and the pointing of each camera relative to the blaze) that only the builders can settle.

## 4. Findings: errors and unsupported statements

### 4.1 The out-of-plane angle γ: size and sign are both unsourced choices

ZSPEC says only "A 0.6° out-of-plane tilt separates the returning dispersed beam from the entrance slit." The preset enters γ = +0.6°, which the explorer treats as the beam's angle to the dispersion plane, so the incoming and returning beams are 2γ = 1.2° apart. The page states this as fact ("the beams are 2γ = 1.2° apart out of the page"). A reading in which the beams are 0.6° apart (γ = 0.3°) is equally consistent with the sentence.

The sign matters more than the size. With γ ≠ 0 the slit images tilt by tan⁻¹(2 tan δ tan γ) = 2.6° (DERIVED: 2 × 2.2045 × tan 0.6° = 0.0461 rad), and the VPH tilts each order by 4.4–8.3°. The explorer's resolving power uses the slit-image band and the dispersion measured normal to the tilted slit image, so the result depends on whether the line tilt adds to or subtracts from the order tilt. In the preset the two tilts add, and Rs drops 0.7% below the closed form. With γ = −0.6° the two tilts partly cancel and Rs rises 0.7% above it:

| channel | γ = +0.6° (preset) | γ = +0.3° | γ = 0 | γ = −0.6° |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B Rs / slit image | 17,677 / 4.492 px | 17,737 / 4.475 | 17,797 / 4.459 | 17,916 / 4.432 |
| YJ Rs / slit image | 17,734 / 4.477 | 17,766 / 4.468 | 17,797 / 4.459 | 17,859 / 4.446 |
| K Rs / slit image | 17,700 / 4.486 | 17,748 / 4.472 | 17,797 / 4.459 | 17,894 / 4.438 |

The research record attributes the 0.7% to γ ("gamma = 0.6 deg with the VPH stage widens the slit image by 0.7%"), which is correct, but the record does not note that the sign of the effect is a free choice. The project's simulator has no γ at all (IRDBT has no such column), so its 18,000 and 4.46 px correspond to the γ = 0 column. Effect: ±0.7% in Rs and in slit sampling; no effect on dispersion, coverage or order spacing. All scored lines pass with either sign.

### 4.2 H channel: order range misattributed (confirmed error)

The H nOrders info line reads "10 orders, 42–51" with src "ZShooter website, spectrograph design page". The website table says "H 1,470–1,830 41–51; 11 orders 300" (zspec_optical.html, "Channels and spectral format"). The 42–51 range comes from the Zemax archive via ZSXD ("H 42-51 24 300 -6.10 -3.00") and the current IRDBT row ("h 4 4 51 10 ..."). The H note also says "orders 42–51" without the conflict. Fix: src "June 2026 Zemax archive via the simulator (ZSXD); the website table says 41–51, 11 orders, from the older simulator". Order 41 has its blaze at 1851 nm (DERIVED: 75.890 µm / 41), outside the 1830 nm passband, so 42–51 is the physically consistent range. Effect: none on R, dispersion or coverage (info line).

### 4.3 "Bailey et al. 2026" cited as a source of the numbers (unsupported)

data/round6/zshooter.md, CRB26: "METADATA ONLY: the paper itself was not obtained". The note lists the paper beside the website and the simulator as a source. The website does cite the manuscript ("ZShooter SPIE 2026 manuscript, §4.1, Table 2, and Figure 5: optical prescription"), so a correct wording is "the website's summary of Bailey et al. 2026", or the citation should be dropped.

### 4.4 "the website quotes f/10.5, but ... the 530.6 µm/″ slit scale [gives] f/10" (misleading)

The slit scale gives f/10 only on the explorer's 10.95 m Keck pupil (530.6 µm/″ × 206,265 = 109.44 m; 109.44/10.95 = f/9.995, DERIVED). The website's own numbers are self-consistent on a smaller pupil: f/14.5 × 500/690 = f/10.51, and 109.44 m / 10.51 = 10.42 m (DERIVED). The NIR cold stop "at the 47.8 mm pupil masks the primary-mirror perimeter" (ZSPEC), which corresponds to f/14.44 at the 690 mm doublet (DERIVED), so f/14.5 plausibly refers to a pupil smaller than the 11 m perimeter (INFERRED). Only the 1500 mm / 150 mm pair independently gives f/10, and that pair defines the beam on the echelle. The note presents a pupil-convention difference as a website error. Effect on outputs: none (Rs depends on f_col and the slit scale, both kept; W = 150 mm is the published beam). Suggested wording: "relayed to the published 530.6 µm/″ (f/10 on the explorer's 10.95 m Keck pupil; the website's f/14.5 → f/10.5 implies a 10.4 m pupil)".

### 4.5 "2026 conceptual design review" (unsupported date)

No saved source dates the CoDR. The IRDB trace table header says "date_created : 2025-10-20", and the earlier Zemax archive 20260225A was a three-channel IR file, so the review could precede 2026. Suggested wording: "the post-CoDR design (June 2026 Zemax archive)".

### 4.6 "off-axis paraboloid" (unsupported qualifier)

ZSPEC says "parabolic collimator". Off-axis use is likely in a white-pupil layout, but no saved source says so. Low severity; drop "off-axis" or mark the qualifier as inferred.

### 4.7 B channel: "the design reaches 3050 Å" (imprecise)

The design passband starts at 310 nm (ZSOD table "VIS B 310–430"). The Zemax order-118 window starts at 3084 Å, and the 3049.9 Å figure is the simulator's on-chip trace edge (ZSDRP). Suggested wording: "the B passband starts at 3100 Å; the explorer stops at 3200 Å".

### 4.8 Three scored lines are circular by the file's own rule

The header of web/presets.js says "info: true marks a reference line that is printed but not scored: circular (the state was derived from the same number)". Three ZShooter lines meet that definition.
- npix 4.46: ZSRES derives the value from the same inputs that set fcam = 270 mm: "0.70×0.5306×225/1250÷0.015 = 4.457 pixels"; IRDBT calls it a "rounded derived nominal value".
- pixScale 0.157: IRDB ZShooter.yaml "pixel_scale : 0.1570545 #"/pix using .7"/4.5 pix", which is 0.015 mm / (0.5306 × 225/1250 mm/″), the same arithmetic.
- ordSpan: the simulator's on-chip span is 2048 px times the same closed-form dispersion (270 mm effective camera, 65.6° blaze); the only independent content is the detector rotation and offset.

The R ≈ 18,000 line is also computed by the simulator from the same 65.6°/1500/1250/225 mm numbers, but the line still tests the explorer's ray trace against the closed form. The minSepPx line is the one semi-independent check: the simulator uses the real VPH rulings with the 225 mm camera and the Zemax CX angles, while the explorer uses the scaled rulings at its own Bragg angle; the two agree to ≤0.7%. Effect on outputs: none. The validation suite currently scores consistency with the inputs and reports the result as agreement with published performance.

### 4.9 Block comment overstates the provenance of the validation targets

Line 325: "Validation targets from the project's simulator (the June 2026 Zemax design)". The targets come from the simulator's analytical planar model, which takes rulings, orders, CX angles and detector rotations from the June 2026 archive but uses the nominal 225/270 mm cameras and detector placements set by hand. ZSXD: "R moves left 150 pixels, H left 130 pixels, and YJ moves up 50 pixels with reddest order 57 removed"; IRDBT: "Neither angle is fitted to detector coverage". No Zemax ray trace enters. The pub src strings for ordSpan and minSepPx say this correctly; the comment does not. Also, the minSepPx values (81.5, 85.7, 112.1, 63.6, 136.2, 225.5 px) are DERIVED by the research from footprint bounding boxes in selected_orders.csv, which the pub line does not mark. I recomputed all six from the CSV and they are right.

## 5. Silent assumptions and best-supported values

### 5.1 The R target: a better source now exists

New source (saved, see section 8): zs-scopesim d28f077 docs/performance-reconciliation.md, "Slit configuration and resolving power": "The corrected .70 plot gives approximately 17.5–18.0k over its plotted detector coverage; .33 gives approximately 37.2–38.3k." and "The old constant 4.46-pixel slit assumption retained changing dispersion without its matching slit magnification and consequently produced the larger 16–21k and 33–45k ranges." and "Their larger geometric-R variation and AO-background mismatch should not be interpreted as instrument performance."

This confirms, in the project's own words, the pub line's claim that the website's 15,700–21,100 range is an artifact. The project's corrected geometric R, 17.5–18.0k, is a tighter target than "≈18,000". The explorer's Rs at the six λc (17,677–17,734) and at on-chip FSR points (17,455–17,921, research record) fall within or at the edge of that range. Suggested pub: value 17,750, tol 0.02, src "zs-scopesim performance-reconciliation.md (corrected simulator, 0.70″: 17.5–18.0k); website median ≈18,000". The website pages (dated 2026-10-16) still show the uncorrected 15,700–21,100. The same note adds "Camera aberrations, non-coplanar coupling and wavelength-dependent optical image quality could change a Zemax line width", and ZSRES says its conclusion "is not evidence that Jason's description of the Zemax model is wrong". The optical designer's Zemax model may therefore show more variation along the order than the planar model does (open question 7.3).

### 5.2 γ (section 4.1)

Best-supported value: the magnitude is 0.3° or 0.6° and the sign is unknown. The γ = 0 column of the table in 4.1 matches the project's simulator.

### 5.3 NIR pixel size

The website baseline is 15 µm, but as a target. The June 2026 Zemax NIR image aperture is 36.864 × 36.864 mm (zemax_channels.csv, "yj,...,36.864,36.864,..."), which is 2048 × 18 µm (DERIVED). The IRDB README lists "18 um H2RGs in YJ/H/K" as the alternative. With 18 µm pixels the 0.70″ slit image becomes 3.71 px (ZSRES: "the same physical image spans 3.714 pixels"), the chip grows by 20% in both axes, and R is unchanged. The preset follows the published baseline; the note could say "15 µm (target)".

### 5.4 Camera focal lengths

Preset: the nominal 225 mm. Zemax paraxial values (zemax_channels.csv, computed by zemax_prescription.py from the archive's surfaces and glass catalog): B 224.49, G 224.29, R 225.70, YJ 225.14, H 224.65, K 222.25 mm. Rerun with fcam = 270 × f_Zemax/225: Rs unchanged; K slit image 4.486 → 4.431 px, 0.2811 → 0.2846 Å/px, 0.1568 → 0.1587″/px, order-34 on-chip span 575.1 → 582.1 Å (+2.0% vs the 570.8 Å target, still inside the 3% tolerance); other channels change by ≤0.3%. The simulator also uses 225 mm, so the nominal value is consistent with the targets; the Zemax values describe the design better.

### 5.5 Where the blaze falls on the chip (camera pointing)

The explorer and the simulator both center the blaze of the central order on the chip in the dispersion direction (θ = 0 and no camera offset in the explorer; the simulator's FSR is blaze-centered, e.g. "b,99,365.0019...,368.7075..." centered on the 366.85 nm blaze). The Zemax archive contains a camera tilt that neither model uses: camera_center_tilt_y_deg = −2.0 (B), −0.75 (G), 0.0 (R), −2.0 (YJ), +1.0 (H), +0.5 (K) (zemax_channels.csv; zemax_prescription.py reads the tilt as "parameters[93, config, 4]", a coordinate-break tilt at the camera).

A related pattern in the archive is new to this audit. The archive samples three orders per channel, and the designer's wavelength windows are anchored on the passband red edges, not on the grating equation. The window centers, (first sample + extrapolated twelfth sample)/2 from zemax_sampling.csv, put the reddest order of each channel exactly on the passband edge: B order 85 at 430.000 nm, G 56 at 650.000, R 37 at 980.000, YJ 57 at 1340.000, H 42 at 1800.000, K 31 at 2450.000 nm (DERIVED). The corresponding mλ (36,550, 36,400, 36,260, 76,380, 75,600, 75,950 nm) differ from the 50.15 or 24 /mm, 65.6° blaze by +0.64%, +0.23%, −0.16%, +0.65%, −0.38% and +0.08% (DERIVED).

The window offsets track the camera tilts. A camera tilt τ in the 125 mm white pupil corresponds to an echelle angle τ/1.2, which moves the wavelength on the camera axis by cos β (τ/1.2)/(sin α + sin β) = 0.33% per degree (DERIVED). The predicted shifts are +0.66% (B, YJ), +0.25% (G) and −0.33% (H), against observed +0.64%, +0.65%, +0.23% and −0.38%. R and K, whose local propagation sign is positive (IRDBT angle_note: "s is negative for B/G/YJ/H and positive for R/K"), fit in sign once the sign is flipped. INFERRED: the June 2026 Zemax cameras are pointed off the blaze in the echelle-dispersion direction, so that each detector centers on the passband-anchored windows.

Cost of ignoring the tilt, if the inference holds (DERIVED, f_cam 225 mm):
- B and YJ: the format shifts by 225 × tan 2.0° = 7.9 mm (≈520 px) along the dispersion. At the chip center β = 67.3° instead of 65.6°, so the dispersion is 6% finer (cos β ratio 0.941), the 0.70″ slit image is 4.74 px instead of 4.46, and R is 0.6% higher.
- H: shift 3.9 mm (≈260 px) in the opposite sense; slit image 4.30 px; dispersion 3.5% coarser.
- G and K: shifts of 2.9 and 2.0 mm.

The tilt does not change R by more than 0.7% anywhere, but the tilt changes which part of each order and of each FSR lands on the chip, the Å/px at the chip center, and the sampling there. The explorer cannot represent a camera pointed off the blaze at fixed incidence: θ splits the angle symmetrically between incidence and diffraction. The axis assignment of a Zemax coordinate break after folds is not certain, so this needs the builders (open question 7.2).

### 5.6 Other assumptions, checked

- VPH angle: the explorer sets each VPH at the symmetric Bragg angle for λc with the scaled ruling (B 17.80°), while the Zemax CX tilts are 21.2°, 16.75°, 14.1°, 10.9°, 13.7° and 16.8°. Those tilts are near the Bragg angles of the real rulings, symmetric at 3616, 5240, 7796, 10806, 15789 and 21410 Å (DERIVED: 2 sin τ / G). Effect: order spacings agree with the simulator to ≤0.7%; no effect on R.
- Detector rotation (5–9.5°) and offsets: not modeled; stated in the note.
- Dichroics: not modeled; stated.
- θ = 0: supported (table in section 2).

## 7. Open questions for the builders

7.1 γ: is the 0.6° the echelle's out-of-plane tilt (beams 1.2° apart) or the angle between the incoming and returning beams (γ = 0.3°)?
