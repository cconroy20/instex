# Audit X: the Interferometer arrays (nine presets)

Scope: vla, alma, dsa2000, arecibo, vlba, eht2017, chime, chimeout, chord, as built by make_rpresets.js into web/rpresets.js from data/interf/*.json, read in the code state of 2026-11-16 (after the DSA haMax 2, the VLA C move of N10 to N1, the EHT "m87" default sky, shadowing, transit drift weights, the Shadowed row). `node validate_int.js` gives 116 pass, 0 fail. No project file was edited. CHIME, CHIME + outriggers and CHORD were audited by two parallel readers; their sections are included unchanged below. VLA, ALMA, DSA, Arecibo, VLBA and EHT were audited by me, with the saved sources. Marks: DERIVED = arithmetic done here; INFERRED = a reading no source states.

## VLBA

Verified correct (quotes from rawdata/interf/vlba/):
- Ten 25 m stations: OSS intro "The VLBA is an array of ten 25-m diameter antennas at stations distributed over United States territory". Positions are OSS Table 4.1 (WGS84), which the record cross-checks to CASA's vlba.cfg (0.2-1.7 m) and to SCHED's GSFC solution (cm). Chords: SC-MK 8611.6 km and LA-PT 236.6 km (OSS Table 8.1 gives 8611 and 236, truncated).
- Bands and ranges: OSS Table 5.1 rows ("21 cm (c) | L | 1.35 - 1.75", "2 cm | U | 12.0 - 15.4", "3 mm (g) | W | 80.0 - 90.0", and so on), bandwidths DERIVED from the recording rates (OSSC "maximum rate of 4096 Mbps"). The 3 mm band at eight stations: Table 5.2 lists NL, FD, LA, PT, KP, OV, BR, MK; no HN or SC row (INFERRED that those lack a receiver; the page says so only as a count).
- 10° practical limit: OSS declination page, "An elevation of 10 degrees is the practical limit for most stations." 20° recommended: "the NRAO recommends observing sources when they have an elevation above about 20 degrees at a participating station". Mechanical 2.25° is SCHED's AX2LIM=2.25,90 for every station. SC's mask maximum 16° is from SCHED's HOR_EL (record); the OSS notes SC sources "get above the horizon ... about 10 degrees", consistent.
- bminNote (Los Alamos-Pie Town 236.64 km) and the 8 h default's source: OSS Table 5.1 text, "image sensitivity values ... assuming ... a total on-source integration time of 8 hours".

Findings, ranked:

1. The 1/ν primary beam is a one-frequency measurement scaled over 0.3-86 GHz (low-medium). Page: pbSource "θ ≈ 40.3′ / ν(GHz): measured at 1.38 GHz (Middelberg et al. 2013) and scaled as 1 / ν". Source: Middelberg Sec. 3.4, "The best-fitting FWHM of the Gaussian model was found to be 29.13 arcmin, and the best-fitting antenna diameter for the Airy disk model was found to be D = 25.47 m." That is 0.977 λ/D for D = 25 m (DERIVED), a narrow beam for a dish with this feed taper (an Airy for uniform illumination gives 1.029 λ/D). No NRAO relation is in any saved VLBA page (grep of the OSS intro, correlator, and Proposing Guide pages for "primary beam" finds only qualitative statements), so the scaling to the 3 mm receiver (different feed, different edge taper) is INFERRED. The page discloses the scaling; it does not disclose that the beam was measured on one antenna set at one frequency and that the taper changes with the receiver. Effect: the Primary beam readout at 86 GHz (28.1″ from the relation) may be off by tens of percent; it only attenuates sources off-axis, so images near the phase center are unaffected. Fix: add "(a single measurement; other receivers' beams differ)".
2. The scored check is looser than its README sentence (low). validate_int requires 44 of 49 cells within 0.2 h and reports "45 of 49, worst 1.0 h"; the README says 45 of 49, but data/interf/vlba.md says "46 of 49 cells within 0.2 h" (two numbers for one test). The test uses the OSS table's definition (elevation of at least 3°, above the local horizon), not the page's 10° default, so it validates the horizon masks and positions, not the page's elevation limit. The threshold 44 sits one below the observed 45, which makes the check pass by construction. Fix: state 45 in the record; say the check tests the masks at 3°.
3. durNote (low): "The default 8 h is the time on the source assumed by the Status Summary's sensitivity table". The OSS says total on-source integration time, which a real track exceeds (calibrators, slews); the page's default is an 8 h track with the source up. Wording only.
4. Elevation limit by band (low): the PROP manual says "If you are observing at very low frequencies (< 1 GHz) or very high frequencies (>40 GHz), the practical elevation limit for each station is closer to about 20 degrees." The page applies 10° for every band, including P, 610 and W; elNote states only the 10° and 20° recommended. Add the band qualifier.
5. The made-up core-jet sky is labeled: index.html's Sky tooltip says "a quasar's core and jet (an illustration)" and interf.js says "(an illustration, not a particular quasar)". It is the VLBA's default sky. Nothing in it is presented as real. Open point: the jet position angle 290° and the fading-knot rule are arbitrary; the default size of 0.01 (10 mas jet length) is within the beam of a 15 GHz VLBI image (0.47 mas, OSS Table 8.2), a sensible choice.

## EHT 2017

Verified correct:
- Eight stations from Paper II Table 2 (X, Y, Z re-parsed by the record to the PDF), phased ALMA 37 × 12 m = 73 m and phased SMA 6 × 6 m = 14.7 m (Paper II Table 3 note c: "The median number of phased antennas in 2017 for the SMA was 6 × 6 m antennas and for ALMA was 37 × 12 m antennas"). SEFDs are Paper III Table 2 medians (ALMA 74, APEX 4800, JCMT 10500, LMT 4500, PV 6900, SMA 6400, SMT 17100, SPT 19300 Jy). Bands: Paper III Sec. 2, "two 2 GHz bands, low and high, centered at sky frequencies of 227.1 and 229.1 GHz"; the edges ±1 GHz are DERIVED.
- Reference meridian: gst0 199.402° + 360.9856°/day × 4.025 h − RA 187.706° = 72.2° (DERIVED here, equal to the page's lon0). Track 0.535-7.515 h UTC and the 8.33 Gλ maximum from the SR1 release, per the record.
- The crescent: Paper IV Appendix C.1, item 2, "A crescent composed of a delta-function ring with radius r0=22 μas and a dipolar angular intensity profile I(r,θ) = I0[1 + 2A cos(θ − η)] ... where I0=0.55 Jy, the asymmetry parameter A=0.23 ... and η is the orientation angle east of north. The crescent is then also convolved with a 10 μas Gaussian." Sec. 9.2: "η=150°". The page's CRESCENT {blur 10/44, A 0.23, pa 150} matches. The new "m87" default adds Table 10's jet; the three components (0.3, 1000 × 600 μas, PA 140°, (−688.77, 80.86); 0.2, 400 × 200, 93°, (−295.44, −52.09); 0.1, 400 × 200, 115°, (−215.80, 208.40)) equal M87.jet in interf.js.
- CLEAN beam: Paper IV Table 1, "April 11 25.4 17.4 6.0"; the page's 23.5 × 16.6 µas, PA 179° (= −1°) is 7-8% narrower and 7° from it, inside the check's 15% and 10°. Uniform-weighted beams depend on the uv cell (known; README).

Findings, ranked:

1. The per-station elevation limits are DERIVED from one track and applied as hard limits at every declination (medium). Page elNote: "ALMA and APEX 20°, the SMT 25° (the M87 tracks of 2017), JCMT 12° and the SMA 16° (the lowest elevations in the released data), the IRAM 30 m 9° and the LMT 0° (mechanical)". Sources: Paper III Sec. 2, "In most tracks, VLBI scans on M87 began when it rose at the LMT and ended when it set below 20° elevation at ALMA." So 20° is ALMA's scheduling cut for the track's end, not a hardware limit (ALMA's own is far lower; the saved ALMA Technical Handbook states no elevation limit, grep for "elevation limit|minimum elevation" finds none). APEX's "20°" is the floor of its detected elevations (20.7° on April 5), which is the ALMA cut seen at the same site: an artifact, not an APEX limit. JCMT 12° and SMA 16° are the lowest elevations among detections (12.5°, 16.3°), set by when those stations joined an ongoing track, an upper bound on their limits. SMT 25° is a local obstruction toward M87: Paper IV note 121, "for the SMT, M87 rises behind the Large Binocular Telescope, so the minimum observable elevation is ∼25°", an azimuth-specific limit the page applies to all azimuths and declinations. Only PV (9°, SCHED AX2LIM=9,89) and LMT (0°, AX2LIM=0,90) are instrument limits. Searches for SMA and JCMT limits found none (web search, 2026-11-16: no SMA observer's-guide figure; one value, 33°, for the SMA subcompact configuration only). Effect: on the default track the numbers reproduce April 11; moving the declination slider cuts JCMT, SMA, APEX and SMT at limits the stations do not have, so the uv coverage for other sources is truncated. elNote says what the numbers are, honestly; the weakness is how they are applied. Fix: say "applied at every declination" in elNote, or use 0-2° mechanical limits and let the declination choose the track.
2. pbSource: "the larger dishes' beams are narrower" is wrong for the phased arrays (low). The beam of a phased array is the single element's: ALMA's 12 m (same as APEX) and the SMA's 6 m, whose 1.2 λ/D beam is about 54″ at 229 GHz (DERIVED), wider than APEX's 27.1″. The 73 m and 14.7 m "dishes" are collecting-area equivalents (Paper III Table 2 note c: "The diameter for phased arrays reflects the sum total collecting area"). The preset's single beam (APEX's) is irrelevant for a 44 µas source. Fix the sentence.
3. Paper IV disagrees with itself on the crescent (low). Sec. 6.1: "a tapered crescent of the same diameter with its brightest point oriented directly south" (180°), and "0.6 Jy for all these simple geometric models"; Appendix C.1 and Sec. 9.2 give η = 150° and I0 = 0.55 Jy. The page follows C.1/9.2 and the record cites them; neither text mentions the discrepancy. The index.html tooltip says "a ring brighter toward the southeast", which fits 150° (E of N) but is loose.
4. The m87 default sky is Paper IV's synthetic model, scaled (low). The jet components of Table 10 are in absolute μas, but the page scales the whole figure (ring and jet) by Size / 44 µas (interf.js m87Jet, `k = size / M87.d`), so changing the Size slider moves the jet with the ring, which the paper's jet does not do. The compact share, 0.55 of 1.15 Jy, is the paper's 0.55 + 0.6. The tooltip does not say that the jet scales with the Size.
5. Defaults (informational): uniform weighting, 6.98 h centered on a transit at 72.2° W: the track is a choice of longitude that makes the SR1 times; no real track is centered on one meridian (the durNote says so).

## Arecibo

Verified: illuminated 213 × 237 m = 700 × 776 ft (users' guide Table 1, "Gregorian Feeds 213 × 237 m (700 × 776 ft)"), 213.36 and 236.52 m DERIVED; zenith limit "Zenith Angle 0◦–19◦.7"; declination "−1◦ 20′ < Dec < +38◦ 02′" (the preset holds −1.3333 and 38.0333, the guide's rounded values); beams "3.1 × 3.5" arcmin (L-wide, 1.42 GHz, azimuth × zenith angle); dates: NSF news release 20-010, "On Aug. 10, 2020, an auxiliary cable failed"; "November 19, 2020 Following a review of engineering assessments ... damage ... cannot be stabilized without risk"; release 20-011, "fell at approximately 7:55 a.m. Atlantic Standard Time Dec. 1". Edge taper −7 dB: the G92-03 design memo (record: "9) edge taper = -7 dB ... 10) rolloff exponent = 6"; a different paper, PHB, says "about 9 db").

Findings:
1. The aperture's orientation is better supported than the record says (low). The preset's comment says the 213 m is north-south on the meridian, INFERRED. The design memo (EXPP, Forward Gain) says "The aperture in this design is elliptical, being larger in the transverse dimension than in the uphill-downhill direction", with the 700 ft (213 m) dimension along the uphill-downhill (zenith-angle) direction; Heiles et al. Sec. 3.1 adds that the za beam is the larger one. On the meridian the zenith-angle direction is north-south, so 236.5 east-west by 213.4 north-south is supported, and the beam (3.1′ az × 3.5′ za) agrees. Off the meridian the patch turns with the azimuth arm; the page's "on the meridian" is the right caveat. Upgrade INFERRED to supported (EXPP).
2. Two illumination statements disagree (low): the design −7 dB power edge taper (page) versus PHB's "geometric taper of about 9 db". The page uses the design value and says "the real illumination tapers to −7 dB"; that sentence presents a design value as measured. Suggested: "is designed to taper to −7 dB".
3. Bands: the preset lists twelve receivers' ranges from the 2017 users' guide and the RXT status page (the record machine-checks them); "430chg" (the carriage-house 430 line feed) is rightly excluded from the list. The C-high range 5.9-8.1 GHz comes from the August 2020 status page, not the 2017 guide, and overlaps C-wide (4-8 GHz); the sources differ in vintage, which the page does not say.
4. The measured first-sidelobe range 3.9-4.6% (Heiles 2001) versus the model's 1.75% is stated in the beamNote. Fine.

### Findings, ranked by severity

**1. The southern declination limit, −5.7°, is unsupported, and the reachNote contradicts itself (high).**
- Page: decRange [−5.7, 90] for both arrays. CHIME reachNote: "Declinations above -5.7°: the beam reaches 55° from the zenith, half its north-south extent, so CHIME sees three quarters of the sky each day as the Earth turns." chimeout: "Declinations above -5.7°, as for CHIME". Below −5.7° the page reports "Within reach: no" and draws no samples ("the source is out of reach"; confirmed in a screenshot at δ = −12.1°).
- The −5.7° is DERIVED in chime.md from FRB18's "N-S FoV ∼110°" (49.32 − 55). No source states the limit.
- CAT1 (new source), Sec. 2: "The CHIME/FRB detector views the entire sky north of declination −11◦ (a configurable choice, see below) every day as it transits overhead. Sources northward of declination +70◦ are visible twice per day". CAT1 Sec. 2: "Formed beams are spaced evenly in sin θ N-S from θ = −60◦ to θ = +60◦". CAT1 Sec. 2.2: "We evaluate the exposure for daily transits of all sky locations with declination δ > −11◦". CAT1 Fig. 1 caption: "outside the CHIME/FRB field of view, below declination −11◦". The ±60° span from the 49.32° zenith gives −10.7° and, at lower transit, δ > +70.7° (DERIVED), so the two CAT1 numbers agree.
- OVW22 Sec. 3.2.1: holography tracks span "−21° to 65° in declination (−70° to 16° in zenith angle)". HOLO24 Table 1 lists holography sources at −5.9 (3C161), −12.1 (Hydra A) and −21.5 (3C358), all of which the page puts out of reach. OVW22 Sec. 3.1 and HOLO24 Sec. 1 give the N-S extent as "∼120 degrees" and "∼ 120◦ wide across the band".
- "Three quarters": OVW22 Sec. 2.2 says CHIME "observes most of the 3/4 of the celestial sphere visible from our site", and App. A gives 31000 deg² (fsky ≈ 0.75), which corresponds to δ > −30.2° (DERIVED). The sky north of −5.7° is 55% (22,700 deg²); north of −11°, 60% (24,600 deg², DERIVED). The note's "three quarters" is wrong for either limit.
- Fix: decRange [−11, 90] for both arrays, with CAT1 as the source; the N-S extent 120° (OVW22, HOLO24, CAT1's ±60° tiling), with FRB18's 110° as an alternative. reachNote, for example: "Declinations above −11°: the CHIME/FRB search's beams span 60° on either side of the zenith along the meridian, approximately 60% of the sky (CHIME/FRB 2021)." If the page wants the cosmology instrument's reach, OVW22's holography gives −21°; state which one the page uses. Effect: sources at −11° ≤ δ < −5.7° become observable; the "three quarters" claim goes. Lower transits (δ > +70°) remain unmodeled; a note would cover them.

**2. CHIME + outriggers models a different array from the one that observes, and the random draw sets the beam (medium).**
- Page: CHIME's 960 feeds stay individual elements next to three phased outriggers: 463,203 baselines, of which 460,320 lie within CHIME (≤ 98 m), natural weighting by default, weights 1/(SEFD_i SEFD_j).
- VLBI26 Sec. 2.1.1: "we use multiple phase center beamforming to form individual beams at the CHIME core and each Outrigger station". CHIME is phased into one station too, so the real VLBI data hold the station-to-station baselines only. VLBI26 Sec. 2 adds that the Outrigger–Outrigger baselines are, "in practice", "not bright enough to exceed the noise threshold".
- Computed here (DERIVED, scratchpad audit/chimesim/beam.js, which samples as intapp.js does): with all pairs, CHIME's internal baselines carry 57.5% of the natural weight, a flat pedestal under the VLBI fringes over the 1″ field. The page's draw (20,408 pairs at the default: all 2,883 pairs to an outrigger plus 17,525 of the 460,320 internal pairs, not reweighted) leaves 4.9% of the weight in the short baselines. The displayed beam therefore depends on the draw size, and neither number describes the real array.
- The "Longest baseline" readout is 3506 km (HCO–GBO, a baseline VLBI26 says is not used), while antNote names GBO at 3,334 km as the longest.
- Fix: phase CHIME into one station as well (SEFD 40,000/960 ≈ 42 Jy by the same rule; INFERRED), which leaves the six station baselines (or the three CHIME–outrigger ones, with the outrigger pairs optional), removes the draw and matches VLBI26. Alternatively, reweight each drawn non-kept pair by total/drawn.

**4. The east-west beam: the relation holds, and the label and check need care (low).**
- Page pbSource: "east-west ≈ 1.0° / ν(GHz), from 2.5° at 400 MHz and 1.3° at 800 MHz; north-south ≈ 110° (CHIME/FRB 2018)". The Primary beam tooltip calls these "the half-power widths of one feed's beam".

## Summary

(a) Confirmed errors, ranked by severity (fix; source; effect)
1. CHIME and CHIME + outriggers: the southern limit −5.7° is derived, unsupported, and contradicted. Fix decRange to [−11, 90] and drop "three quarters of the sky" (it is about 60%). Source: CHIME/FRB catalog (rawdata/interf/chime/arxiv_2106.04352v3.pdf), "views the entire sky north of declination −11◦"; OVW22 holography reaches −21°.
