# Audit I1: space imager presets (acs, uvis, wfc3ir, euclid, roman, nircamsw, nircamlw, tess)

Auditor: Claude (Opus 5.5), 2026-11-16. Scope: the eight presets in `web/ipresets.js` lines 95-155 and 305-316, against `data/imaging/hst.md`, `space_wide.md`, `tess.md`, `constants.md` and the saved sources under `rawdata/imaging/`. No project file was edited. Text of the saved PDFs was extracted with `pdftotext -layout` into the scratchpad (`audit/txt_I1/`); quotes below were checked against that text unless marked otherwise. `node validate_img.js` passes (369 pass, 0 fail). Model numbers come from `audit/i1_run.js` (scratchpad), which loads each preset exactly as `applyPreset` does (`IM.DEF` overlaid with `p.state`). Screenshots of six presets are in `audit/shots_I1/`.

Labels: DERIVED = arithmetic done here; INFERRED = an interpretation not stated in a source.

## 1. Cross-cutting findings

### 1.1 HST drawing geometry uses defaults the OTA prescription contradicts (acs, uvis, wfc3ir)

The three HST presets set `tel: "cass"` but no `F1` or `back`, so the drawing takes `IM.DEF`: primary f/2.0 and the focus 0.2 D = 0.48 m behind the primary. The page labels the HST primary "Ø 2.40 m, f/2.00" (screenshot `acs.png`).

OTA Handbook (Burrows 1990), Table 2.1 "OTA Optical Design Prescription", printed p. 25 [PDF p. 31]:

> "Primary Mirror Radius 11040. mm ... Secondary Mirror Radius 1358. mm ... Spacing 4906.071 mm ... f/Number 24 ... Aperture 2400. mm ... Vertex Back Focus 1500.129 mm ... Back Focal Distance 6406.200 mm ... Magnification 10.435 ... Exit pupil to focal plane 7003.51 mm"

DERIVED: primary f = 11040/2 = 5520 mm, F1 = 5520/2400 = 2.30 (= 24/10.435); back = 1500.129/2400 = 0.625 D. With `F1: 2.30, back: 0.625` the model reproduces the prescription exactly: secondary spacing 4906.1 mm (published 4906.071), secondary focal length -679.0 mm (R/2 = 679), back focal distance 6406.2 mm (6406.200), exit pupil to focus 7002.6 mm (7003.51). With the defaults the model draws spacing 4394-4494 mm, secondary f = -326 to -443 mm, and exit pupil to focus 5276 mm (25% short).

Effect: drawing only for acs and uvis. For wfc3ir the exit-pupil distance also sets where the reimager's pupil is drawn (f_col + f_col²/L: 364.4 mm past the collimator now, 359.0 mm with the published L). Fix: add `F1: 2.30, back: 0.625` to all three presets; for acs and uvis (F = 25.78, 32.5) the secondary then lands within 1% of 4906 mm.

### 1.2 ACS and UVIS as "direct": the Telescope panel misstates the OTA

In direct mode the Telescope panel reports the focal ratio, focal length and plate scale of the OTA plus the channel's corrector as if they were the telescope's: f/25.8, 61.9 m, 3.33"/mm (acs) and f/32.5, 78.0 m, 2.64"/mm (uvis). The HST Primer OTA table (`primer_cycle34_...optical-performance....html`) gives "Focal Ratio f /24 Plate Scale (on axis) 3.58 arcsec/mm". A builder reading the Telescope panel sees a different telescope. The notes say "modeled as direct" but do not say that the telescope readouts include the channel optics. Fix: one sentence in each note, or model both as reimaged at F = 24 with an explicitly invented beam W (no ACS or UVIS pupil size is published; hst.md "Not found").

### 1.3 "Detector: CCD" shown for HgCdTe arrays (wfc3ir, roman, nircamsw, nircamlw)

The Detector panel's toggle reads "CCD | Plate" and highlights CCD for the WFC3/IR HgCdTe array, the Roman H4RG-10s and the NIRCam HAWAII-2RGs (screenshots `wfc3ir_inst.png`, `roman.png`, `nircamsw.png`). A detector builder would object. UI label, low severity.

### 1.4 Field readout is a bounding box, not "through its center" (roman)

`imager.js` computes `ext` as the bounding box of the chips, while the Field tooltip says "across the mosaic through its center". For Roman the column shifts make these differ: the page shows 48.4' × 30.1'; through the center (the two unshifted columns) the mosaic spans 24.6' (DERIVED: 3 × 40.88 + 7.68 + 2.98 = 133.3 mm at f = 18.644 m). RDox "Description of the WFI": "an outer dimension of the field of view of about 2800 arcsec by 1400 arcsec, including gaps" (= 46.7' × 23.3'). The model's 30.1' matches the SIAF bounding box with the arc (2899" × 1768", space_wide.md DERIVED) and contradicts the tooltip.

### 1.5 Off-axis fields drawn on axis (euclid, roman, nircam)

Euclid (R16: "a Korsch telescope design with a FoV offset of 0.47°"), Roman (RDD: "the WFI focal plane is off-axis") and NIRCam (JDox field-of-view page) use off-axis parts of the telescope field. The model centers the field on the axis. Harmless for the numbers of a direct imager; none of the three notes says so.

- ACSIHB Table 7.11 (p. 204): 0.049313, 0.048651, 0.049895, 0.050328 (mean 0.049547), with the caveat on the same page "These should not be considered as true calibrations".

f/26 with 15 µm gives 0.04958"/px (DERIVED), within 0.2% of the chip-center mean. Best-supported: F = 26.0 (Tiny Tim f/26; or 26.02 from the 0.0495 mean). Effect: pixel scale 0.0500 → 0.0496"/px (-0.8%); fieldX 204.8" → 203.1" (published 202"; the info line's error drops from 1.4% to 0.5%).

With f/26 published and matching the measured scales (A1), the note should cite f/25 (Clampin 2002; Sirianni 2005) and f/26 (Tiny Tim) as published values and drop the explanation, or label the explanation INFERRED.

DERIVED / INFERRED / silent values: F (DERIVED); gap mm (DERIVED from 50 px; Primer gives 750 µm directly); field circle 10' (silent; encloses the 4.9' mosaic diagonal); F1 and back (silent defaults, see 1.1); band I and diffraction-only image (silent choices; the CCD's charge diffusion is not modeled).

## 4. HST / WFC3/IR (wfc3ir)

Verified: one 1014 × 1014 active (1024 × 1024 total), 18 µm array (WFC3IHB Sec. 5.6.1); F = 24 (OTA); W = 14.26 mm from ISR 2014-10 p. 10: "The TinyTIM model expresses the properties of the cold mask in units of the OTA pupil radius, which by design is 7.13mm at the cold mask"; f_eff 29.0 m from W3CDR slide 9 "OTA + WFC3 focal length = 29,000 mm". Model 0.12803"/px vs the geometric mean of 0.135 × 0.121 (0.1278): +0.18%.

**Finding W1 (error, medium): the IR channel's stop is the cold mask, but the preset uses the OTA's D and ε.**
- ISR 2008-14, p. 7: "The WFC3 IR channel has a cold stop that introduces additional obscuration of the pupil. We have included this effect and modelled it as a circular obscuration of the outside of the pupil, a larger circular central obstruction and wider spiders ... The central obscuration radius was 0.354, the outer radius of the cold mask 0.9755 and the spider width 0.061, all expressed in units of the radius of the HST primary at the pupil."
- ISR 2014-10, Table 1, p. 6 (on-orbit fit): "Secondary Radius 0.354 0.370 0.007 ... Spider Width 0.061 0.077 0.001"; "Units are fraction of the OTA primary mirror pupil radius as re-imaged at the cold pupil mask."

Best-supported (DERIVED): D = 0.9755 × 2.4 = 2.341 m; ε = 0.354/0.9755 = 0.363 (design) or 0.370/0.9755 = 0.379 (on-orbit fit). To keep the physical optics unchanged set F = 57.6/2.341 = 24.60, W = 0.9755 × 14.26 = 13.91 mm, Fc = 29.0/2.341 = 12.39; f_col (342.2 mm) and f_cam (172.3 mm) stay the same. Effect: collecting area 4.03 → 3.74 m² (design) or 3.69 m² (fit), -7% to -9%, and the étendue with it; diffraction FWHM at H 0.1378" → 0.1397" / 0.1389" (+1%).

**Finding W2 (misleading text): "a cold mask at the reimaged pupil (radius 7.13 mm)".** 7.13 mm is the radius of the OTA pupil image at the mask (quote above). The mask's own outer radius is 0.9755 × 7.13 = 6.96 mm (DERIVED).

**Finding W3 (unsupported attribution): "the published f/11 gives a pixel 10% too coarse (the 24° detector tilt)".** f/11 gives 0.1406"/px (DERIVED). The tilt foreshortens one axis only (WFC3IHB App. B.1: "The IR detector has a ~24° tilt about its x-axis"), so the geometric-mean scale is finer than the untilted-axis scale by sqrt(0.135/0.121) = 1.056 (DERIVED). The untilted x scale 0.135"/px (App. B.3) still implies f/11.46, 4.2% from f/11. The tilt therefore explains approximately half of the 10%; the rest is a discrepancy between f/11 and the published scales that no source explains.

**Finding W4 (unsupported drawing): "pupil and filter" at the pupil, and a lens collimator and camera.** The page draws a collimator lens (f 342 mm), "pupil and filter Ø 14.3 mm" and a camera lens (f 172 mm), with the caption "the bundles cross at the pupil, where the filter sits". The real relay is two mirrors plus a refractive corrector plate (WFC3IHB Sec. 7.3: "a two-mirror mechanism for providing focus and alignment adjustments; the Refractive Corrector Plate (RCP) ... the IR filter wheel"), and no source places the filter at the pupil (hst.md "Not found": "a text statement that the filter wheel sits at the pupil"). The note should say the collimator and camera are ideal equivalents DERIVED from W and f_eff.

**Finding W5 (wording): "One 1014 × 1014 detector (active area of 1024 × 1024)"** reads as if 1024 × 1024 were the active area. Suggested: "the active 1014 × 1014 pixels of a 1024 × 1024 array".

Scored check: pixScale 0.1278 is labeled as a geometric mean (DERIVED), not circular. Silent: field circle 10'; F1 and back (1.1).

Number of columns (width) 4096"; 36 CCDs, 6 × 6; "Active Area dimensions [mm] 336.6 302.7 ... [deg or deg2] 0.775 0.697 0.540". **Finding E1 (error, low-medium): two scored checks are circular.**
- "0.466 deg² of active silicon", src "Euclid VIS paper": the paper prints no 0.466. The value is DERIVED (36 × 46.6 arcmin²), and EC2's 46.6 arcmin² per CCD is the active area in mm converted with the same 120.57 µm/" that set F (49.6 mm and 49.2 mm → 6.85' × 6.79').

**Finding E2 (error in the note): "the detectors sit at the focus of a Korsch three-mirror telescope, with no optics between".** EC2 Sec. 3, PDF p. 9: "A pupil image is formed part-way along this converging beam, and at this point a dichroic separates the beam by wavelength, with wavelengths λ < 0.95 µm reflected via a third flat fold mirror to the VIS focal plane." Gaspar Venancio et al. 2014 (new source, PDF p. 7): "Three flat mirrors (FOM1 to FOM3) are used to fold the optical combination below the optical bench: FOM1 & FOM2 are between M2 and M3, while FOM3 is between the dichroic plate and the VIS focal plane." EC2 adds that "Fold Mirrors 1 and 2 and the dichroic have complex multilayer dielectric coatings which act to shape the passbands". Fix: "with no powered optics between (a dichroic at the exit pupil and a fold mirror shape the band)". Gaspar Venancio 2014 (PDF p. 4) does support "direct": "The telescope images the sky directly on the VIS focal plane."

**Finding E3 (silent default, low): primary f/2.0.** The preset sets no F1, so the page draws "Ø 1.20 m, f/2.00" (screenshot `euclid.png`). No saved source gives the M1 focal length (space_wide.md "NOT FOUND"; the newly saved Gaspar Venancio 2014 and 2023 papers do not either). R16 Table 5 gives "M1-M2 distance 1756 mm"; the drawing puts the secondary at 0.9 f1 = 2160 mm. Fix: F1 = 1756/(0.9 × 1200) = 1.626 (DERIVED from the drawing's own convention) so that the drawn M1-M2 distance matches the published value, and say in the note that the primary's focal ratio is unpublished.

**Finding E4 (unsupported word): "as-built focal length (24.87 m)".** EC2 lists 24.87 m under "Optical parameters" without calling the value as-built or measured. Label "EC2's focal length" or INFERRED.

**Finding E5 (band, low): band I (0.802 µm) for a 550-920 nm passband** (EC2: "passband from 550–920 nm"). The band center is 0.735 µm (DERIVED); the menu's i (0.749 µm) is closer. Effect: diffraction FWHM 0.134" → 0.125" (-7%).

DERIVED / INFERRED / silent: F (DERIVED from 24.87 m), ε (DERIVED from a PSF-model input), gaps (DERIVED), F1 (silent), field circle 70' (silent; encloses the 62.5' diagonal).

## 8. TESS / one camera (tess)

Verified: D 105 mm (R15 "entrance pupil diameter of 10.5 cm"), f = 146 mm (IH Table 2.1 "Custom 146 mm, f/1.4 lens"; C15S slide 2 "FL, f/# 146 mm, f/1.4"), F = 1.3905 (DERIVED), ε 0, four 2048 × 2048, 15 µm CCDs, rows [2, 2]; gaps 1.74 / 1.58 mm DERIVED from the tess-point in-flight geometry (IH on sky: "The gaps between the CCDs, both vertically and horizontally, subtend 36’ to 41’"; model 40.9' / 37.3'). In-flight focal lengths 145.95-146.04 mm (tess-point) confirm the scale.

**Finding T1 (error, medium): the 24" blur reproduces "≈50% in one pixel" for a Gaussian, but the explorer draws a Moffat.** tess.md derives 24" from a Gaussian ("50% in a = 1 px gives sigma = 0.475 px, FWHM = 1.12 px = 23.7""). `imager.js` and `imgapp.js` draw the "optics" image as a Moffat with β = 4.765. DERIVED (numerical integration over a centered 21.19" pixel): Moffat β 4.765 with FWHM 24.05" puts 41% in the pixel; 50% needs FWHM ≈ 21"; R15 Table 1's second figure ("90% within 60 × 60 µm (4 × 4 pixels)") needs ≈ 40". In flight, IH Sec. 6.7.1: "the fraction of the total charge in the peak pixel: this value generally ranges from 0.2 to 0.4 in the TESS images", which corresponds to Moffat FWHM ≈ 26-35" for a centered star (DERIVED). Fix: either blur 21" (design, "≈50% in one pixel") or ≈30" (in-flight typical), and say which in the note. The note should also say the 50% figure is the design value averaged over the field (R15 Sec. 4.1: "averaged over the FOV").

**Finding T2 (misleading note and src text): the model already includes the gnomonic falloff, and gives 24.4°, not 24.8°.**
- The `fieldX` pub src reads "the model's linear scale gives about 24.8°". The model computes field angles with atan and reports 1465' = 24.42° (validate output), 1.0% above the in-flight 24.17° (tess.md DERIVED from tess-point). The 24.79° figure in tess.md assumed a linear (radian) mapping that the explorer does not use.
- The note says "a corner pixel covers 21% less sky than a central one, which the model leaves out". The model's flat-detector f tan θ mapping already gives a corner pixel cos³θ = 0.876 of the central solid angle (12.4% less, DERIVED at r = 44.6 mm). The model omits the lens distortion beyond that, ≈10% at the corner. Integrated over the field, the model gives 554.9 deg² vs 538 deg² in flight (+3.1%), not tess.md's 581.4 deg² "linear model".
- Field circle 2110' (35.2°) was chosen so that the "linear model's mosaic corners (35.02 deg)" fit; under the model's atan mapping the corners sit at 33.98° (DERIVED). The published "34 degrees diagonal field" (C15 abstract) encloses the mosaic and could be used (2040').

**Finding T3 (misattributed quote): 'Ricker+15 ("21 arcseconds")'.** R15 gives "0.35 arcmin" (Sec. 4.1, PDF p. 5; Table 1, PDF p. 7); the words "21 arcseconds" are the HEASARC page's ("1 detector pixel or 21 arcseconds (approx. 0.35 arcmin) on sky"). "f = 146 mm from Chrisp+15": in the saved C15 paper the parameter table is an image; the text is in the C15S slides and IH Table 2.1. The 21" check (tolerance 1.5%, model +0.91%) tests a value published only to 0.005' = 0.3" (1.4%); not a sharp test.

**Finding T4 (omission): in-flight image quality.** The note gives the design "≈50% of a star's light in one pixel" and not the in-flight 0.2-0.4 peak fraction (T1).

DERIVED: F, gaps, blur, field circle. tess-point's own caveat (quoted in tess.md): "hard coded focal plane geometry is not up to date and may contain inaccurate results."

## 10. Open questions only the builders could answer

1. ACS/WFC and WFC3/UVIS: the pupil diameter at IM2 and the UVIS corrector, and the relay focal lengths (Woodruff & Cahill 1998; Turner-Valle et al. 2004; both paywalled, not obtained). Needed only if the presets move to reimaged mode.
2. ACS/WFC: the f-ratio the instrument team regards as the beam value (f/25 in Clampin 2002 and Sirianni 2005, f/26 in Tiny Tim).
3. WFC3: as-built effective focal lengths (only the December 2000 CDR's 78,000 mm and 29,000 mm are published), and whether the IR filter wheel sits at the pupil.
4. Euclid: the M1 radius of curvature (not in Gaspar Venancio 2014 or 2023, or R16), and whether EC2's 24.87 m is a measured or as-designed value.
5. Roman: the as-built WFI focal length or plate scale. RDox says "nearly constant at 110 mas per pixel", the 2022 SIAF gives 0.10932 × 0.10640"/px for all 18 detectors, and S15's f/7.9 refers to a 2.4 m aperture. Also the basis of RDI's 4.5 m² collecting area, and the as-built central obscuration (P18: Phase B "minimizing the size of the central obscuration", no number).
6. NIRCam: the collimator and camera focal lengths (H05 gives prescriptions, not EFLs) and the as-built pupil diameter.
7. TESS: the measured ensquared energy in flight as a function of field angle, which would set the blur better than the design average.
