## 1A. X-Shooter  { split:"telescope", UVB R / VIS R / NIR T; cut 5595 w 300 aoi 15; cut 10240 w 400 aoi 15 }

CONFIRMED
- Crossovers 559.5 and 1024 nm: V11 Sec. 3.2: "The dichroic crossover between UVB-VIS and VIS-NIR is at 559.5 nm and 1024 nm respectively".
- AOI 15 deg for both: V11 Sec. 2.2.5: "The first dichroic at an incidence angle of 15 deg reflects more than 98% of the light between 350 and 543 nm and transmits ~95% of the light between 600 and 2300 nm. The second dichroic, also at 15 deg incidence, has a reflectivity above 98% between 535 nm and 985 nm and transmits more than 96% of the light between 1045 and 2300 nm." (also V11 Sec. 2.1 "used at 15 deg rather than 45 deg to minimize polarization effects"; XSM Sec. 2.2.1.5 repeats it.)
- Order and reflect/transmit: dichroic 1 reflects UVB (350-543), transmits the rest; dichroic 2 (second in the beam, "sequential use of two dichroics", V11 Sec. 2.1) reflects VIS and transmits NIR. Tree is right: R="xshuvb", T={R:"xshvis", T:"xshnir"}. V11 Fig. 4 caption: "blue: reflection on the first dichroic; orange: transmission through the first dichroic and reflection on the second; red: transmission through both."
- The dichroics are "the first optical elements encountered by the science light (unless the IFU is deployed)" (V11 Sec. 2.2.5) and each arm has its own slit unit with its own slit (V11 Sec. 2.3.1; Sec. 2.1: single shared slit "rejected"). So "split ahead of the slits, each arm with its own slit" is right.
- Shared controls (D only): right. Every arm has its own collimator, slit, ADC/focal reducer and camera (V11 Secs. 2.3-2.5). Telescope D is the only shared quantity. (The preset D = 8.115 m is the 8115 mm pupil; fine.)

ERRORS / FINDINGS
F1.1 (minor, drawing statement). "In the telescope's converging beam" is not strictly right. The dichroics come AFTER the Cassegrain focal plane. V11 Sec. 2.2.1-2.2.5 put the order: shutter and calibration unit (f/13.6 relay) -> A&G slide, which holds the focal-plane mirror, pinhole, IFU ("a 0.5 arcsec pinhole ... placed in the focal plane") -> dichroic box ("the first optical elements encountered by the science light (unless the IFU is deployed)" and V11 Sec. 2.1 "two dichroics after the focal plane"). So the dichroics stand in the DIVERGING f/13.4 beam beyond the focus (DERIVED from the stated order; V11 never uses the word "diverging"). Each arm then has pre-slit optics before its slit: tip-tilt fold, and in UVB and VIS a focal reducer + ADC (f/13.41 -> f/6.5, V11 Sec. 2.2.7), and in the NIR a warm relay (M1 cylindrical, M2 spherical, window, cold stop, M3/M4; V11 Sec. 2.5.1). The page draws a cone converging straight to each slit at the telescope f/#, with the slits at equal paths from dichroic 1. Fix: say "the dichroics stand in the beam from the telescope, ahead of each arm's pre-slit relay and slit" and note that the slit planes are at f/6.5 (UVB, VIS) and f/13.3 (NIR), not at the telescope's f/13.4. The drawn cone's f/# is Ftel = max over arms of P.F = NIR F = 13.285 (preset, from ESO's physical model), within 1% of the published 13.41 (V11 Sec. 2.2.7; the calibration relay simulates f/13.6, V11 Sec. 2.2.1), so the label is harmless.
F1.2 (minor, unsourced numbers). w = 300 and 400 A are in no source. What the sources give: V11 edges 98%R to 543 nm and 95%T from 600 nm (dichroic 1: 57 nm = 570 A wide, DERIVED); 98%R to 985 nm and 96%T from 1045 nm (dichroic 2: 60 nm = 600 A, DERIVED). XSM Sec. 3 (crossover text): "the combined dichroics transmit less than 80% between 556.0-563.8 nm" (78 A) "and between 1009.5-1035 nm" (255 A). Neither matches 300/400 cleanly. Suggest w from the XSM windows, or the V11 98%/95% edges, and say which in the src. The page draws w as a band centered on the crossover, so the V11 edges (543-600 centered 571, not 559.5) also show the band is not symmetric.
F1.3 (moderate, finding per the brief). The arm presets' bands extend well past the crossovers: UVB band [2936, 5930] (upper end = order 13's max, V11 Table 3: 593.0 nm) is 335 A past the 5595 crossover; VIS [5253, 10489] starts 342 A below it and ends 249 A above 10240; NIR starts at 9827, 413 A below 10240. The preset note says the band "is set by the dichroics", but the numbers are the order extents of V11 Table 3 (orders 24-13; 30-16; 26-11), not dichroic limits. The sources give the real limits: XSM Sec. 1 "UV-Blue spectrograph covers the 300-550 nm"; "VIS 550-1000 nm"; V11 Table 1 / Sec. 3.2 crossovers 559.5 / 1024 nm. Where the dichroics drive the arm, the UVB light above ~600 nm (95% transmitted) and VIS below ~535 nm (first dichroic transmits <50% there) should be dropped; the app's All-arms coverage strip therefore shows overlaps 525-593 nm and 983-1049 nm that are far wider than the ~8 nm and ~26 nm dips of XSM Sec. 3. This is a real physical overlap only in the sense that a few percent leaks (XSM: "VIS order 30 gets some flux since the dichroics still reflect and transmit ~15% of the light at 550 nm"). Fix options: filter each arm's band to within the dichroic's 50% points plus the transition (UVB to ~5750, VIS 5350-10500), or keep and add to the caption that the bands are order extents and the dichroic efficiency is not applied.
F1.4 (minor). Nothing is marked inferred; correct, since every number except w is published.

## 1B. MIKE  { split:"telescope", cut 4950 w 400 aoi 45 noAoi:true, R mikeblue, T mikered }

CONFIRMED
- Crossover ~495 nm: LCO manual (Overview): "This configuration is set by the cross over wavelength of the dichroic (4950A)"; Updates, May 2004: "A new dichroic was installed which has a crossover wavelength of ~495 nm. (The old dichroic produced a crossover wavelength of ~455nm.)".
- Reflects blue, transmits red: B03 Sec. 2.1: "a 4mm thick glass window with a dichroic coating on the first surface that reflects blue light and transmits red"; LCO: "a dichroic which reflects (transmits) light into the blue (red) arms". Tree R=mikeblue, T=mikered is right.
- w = 400 A matches the ORIGINAL dichroic's 80%/80% span: B03 Sec. 2.1 "80% reflectance by 440nm and 80% transmission by 480nm" (480-440 = 40 nm, DERIVED). The 2004 495 nm dichroic's width is not published (INFERRED to be similar). Mark the src "B03 (the 455 nm dichroic)" and inferred for the new one.

ERRORS
E1 (SEVERE for the drawing, a published value marked unpublished). The AOI IS published. B03 Sec. 2.1: "The dichroic is positioned in the optical path so that the incidence angle of the beam is 30 degrees from normal. Angles less than 45 degrees are preferable to minimize polarization effects and to keep the crossover interval to a minimum." The new 2004 dichroic goes in the same slide ("The dichroic is trivial to replace"), so 30 deg stays (INFERRED for the 2004 part). So aoi must be 30 and noAoi removed. The beam is then turned 180 - 2x30 = 120 deg, not 90 deg; this agrees with B03 Sec. 2.3: "the dichroic reflects the beam back towards the mounting flange" (a 90 deg fold would not send it back). The comment above the node ("its angle is not published") and the README's list "(PFS, 4MOST HRS, LLAMAS, KCWI, MIKE, UVES)" of drawn-at-45 dichroics must change. The src is the 2003 SPIE manuscript, not only the LCO manual.
E2 (SEVERE for the model). MIKE does NOT have two slits. There is ONE slit, and the dichroic is "the first optical element in MIKE beyond the slit" (B03 Sec. 2.1; Fig. 1 "Elements are as follows (from the slit): dichroic slide, field lens, flat mirror, injection triplet, camera..." for the red; "from the slit: field lens, flat mirror, injection triplet..." for the blue, with "The dichroic reflection is omitted from the diagram of the blue side"; Fig. 4 "path of light from the slit to the dichroic, reflecting into the blue side and transmitting into the red"). B03 Sec. 2.3: "The conversion from F/11 (telescope output) to F/3.3 and F/3.6 for the camera/collimators is accomplished using a set of small injection optics, which immediately follow the dichroic". Also LCO: the observer's selectable parameter is "the slit width" (one). So: telescope focus = the slit (shared) -> diverging f/11 beam -> dichroic -> per-arm field lens, flat, injection triplet -> the arm's own double-pass lens. arms.js (README: "each arm has its own slit") is wrong for MIKE. Consequences: (i) slitW and nObj must be SHARED for MIKE (SHARED_TEL has m: [], so editing slitW in one arm leaves the other arm's slit different, which the real instrument cannot do); (ii) the drawing should show one slit ahead of the dichroic; (iii) the caption must say MIKE's dichroic stands after the slit. The split is still "telescope" (before collimation), not "collimated" (the beams are not collimated until the lens's second pass); but it is a third kind: shared slit, dichroic after it.
E3 (moderate). The telescope cone is drawn at f/3.7 (Ftel = max P.F = blue F 550/150 = 3.67), but the telescope output is f/11 (B03 Sec. 2.3; also B03 fiber sec. "converts the F/11 beam to F/3.5"). The preset F is the lens's f/# (the f/# at which the slit image sits after the injection optics, the "virtual image of the slit in the focal plane of each camera", B03 Sec. 2.3). All-arms should draw the telescope at f/11 (Clay Nasmyth; the M2FS preset already carries telF: 11). The drawn cone is 3x too fat.
E4 (minor). The arm presets' bands: blue lamC 3900 covers LCO "3350-5000 A"; red "4900-9500" (the preset says orders 37-70, 4900-9300). These straddle 4950 by only ~50 A: fine.

## 1C. UVES  { split:"telescope", cut 4500 w 300 aoi 45 noAoi:true, R uvesb, T uvesr }

CONFIRMED
- Crossover 450 nm for DIC1: UM Sec. 2.2.1 "Dichroic#1 has a cross-over wavelength at 450 nm, Dichroic#2 at 550 nm"; UM Sec. 3.1 "DICHROIC1: ... cross-over wavelength 450 nm; wavelength ranges: 300-400 and 500-800 nm"; "DICHROIC2 ... 550 nm; 400-500 and 600-1100". DIC1 390+580 is a standard setting (UM Table 3.1).
- Reflects blue, transmits red: UM Sec. 2.1 "Dichroic: This element in the UVES mode selector reflects all the light below a certain wavelength and is transparent for longer wavelengths"; Sec. 2.2.1 "the beam is split into a red (going straight) and blue path (reflected to the right)". Tree is right.
- Each arm has its own slit and slit viewer: UM Sec. 2.2.1 "The light beam from the telescope is focussed on the red arm entrance slit or is directed to the blue arm slit by a mirror"; slit viewers "in front of the spectrograph slits".
- Shared controls D only: right for the slits and collimators, which are separate; but note the shared PRE-slit elements (derotator, ADC, depolarizer, pupil stop, image slicer, filter) are common to both arms (UM Sec. 2.2.1); not drawn.

ERRORS / FINDINGS
U1 (SEVERE for the drawing's premise). The UVES dichroic is NOT in the telescope's converging beam. Dk00 p. 536: "The preslit optics relay the F/15 Nasmyth focal plane to the F/10 red and blue slit planes of the spectrograph ... The diverging F/15 beam from the Nasmyth focal plane enters a fused silica Abbe-Koenig type derotator prism ... To the exit face of this prism a Silica/CaF2 triplet is cemented that collimates the beam. Doublets re-image the Nasmyth focal plane on the slits at F/10". Dk00 Fig. 3 caption (p. 537): "In the collimated beam behind the derotator at the pupil image of the telescope, a slightly oversized or undersized stray light stop can be inserted. Other options are a filter wheel ..., a rotating superachromatic plate to depolarize the light, an ADC ... and a blue mirror or dichroic. The collimated beam is focused on the slits by a red or blue doublet lens." UM Sec. 2.2.1: "At the position of the mode selector (which includes the dichroic filters) the beam is split into a red (going straight) and blue path". So the dichroic is in the COLLIMATED pre-slit beam (a pupil-image beam), and each arm focuses it onto its own slit with its own doublet. Split type: after the shared pre-slit collimator, before the arms' slit doublets and slits (a hybrid: collimated split with separate slits). arms.js labels it "telescope" and draws a converging cone to the slits. Fix options: a new split kind, or draw a collimated pre-slit beam of the UVES pre-slit beam diameter (not published in the sources read) with a doublet in each arm. Telescope label: the drawn "telescope f/10" is really the slit-plane ratio f/10 after the doublets (Dk00), while the telescope is f/15 (UM Sec. 2.2.1: "the converging f/15 beam coming from the telescope"). The preset's D = 8.0 m, F = 10 is the model's convention (the preset note says it reproduces the scales), so the cone is a stand-in, which the caption should say.
U2 (moderate). AOI: not published in UM, Dk00 or Messenger 99 (checked every "dichroic" hit). UM says the blue path is "reflected to the right" of the straight red path, i.e. a ~90 deg fold, which implies ~45 deg incidence (INFERRED). Keep noAoi, but tighten the comment to "the blue path is reflected to the right (UM Sec. 2.2.1), a 90 deg turn, so 45 deg INFERRED". Not settled.
U3 (minor). w = 300 A. UM Sec. 3.1 (text under Table 3.1): "in the region where the dichroic behavior changes from reflection to transmission (430-465 nm)" (35 nm = 350 A, for DIC1 390+564). Use 350 and cite it; the dichroic's own efficiency curves are in the ETC database (not read).
U4 (minor). The arm presets: uvesb coverage 3260-4540 A (390 setting; DIC1 blue 326-454 nm in the manual), 40 A past 4500, inside the 430-465 nm transition: consistent. uvesr starts at 4806: fine.

PART 2. Feature 2, X-Shooter "4c"
======================================================================

README text: "'4c' (X-Shooter): the same prisms, then the collimator again forms the cross-dispersed spectrum below the slit; a flat sends it to a camera that re-images it." Caption labels (build3dFolded): "collimator, passed twice", "prism(s), passed twice", "flat", "camera", and the code comment "X-Shooter's 4C design".

CONFIRMED (the arrangement is right in kind)
- D06 Sec. 3.2: "The three arms are all based on the 4C layout (Collimator Compensation of Camera Chromatism), a variation of the white pupil layout". MSG120 (p. 2): "dubbed 4C (for Collimator Correction of Camera Chromatism). An important advantage of 4C is the small and simple camera and the fact that prisms are used in double pass".
- V11 Sec. 2.3.2 (UVB): "Light from the entrance slit ... feeds a 5 deg off-axis Maksutov-type collimator through a folding mirror. The collimator consists of a spherical mirror and a diverging fused silica corrector lens ... The collimated beam passes through a 60 deg silica prism twice to gain enough cross-dispersion. ... After dispersion, the collimator creates an intermediate spectrum near the entrance slit, where a second folding mirror has been placed. This folding mirror acts also as a field mirror. Then a dioptric camera (4 lens groups ...) re-images the cross-dispersed spectrum at f/2.7".
- VIS (V11 Sec. 2.4.2): identical collimator; "a 49 deg Schott SF6 prism in double pass"; camera of three lens groups at f/2.8.
- NIR (V11 Sec. 2.5.3): "the collimator is made of two spherical mirrors M6 and M7 plus an Infrasil corrector lens ... three prisms are used in double pass. Prism 1 is a 35 deg top angle made of Infrasil; prisms 2 and 3 are two 22 deg top angle ZnSe prisms ... After dispersion, the collimator creates an intermediate spectrum near the entrance slit, where M8, a spherical mirror, acts as a field mirror, relocating the pupil between L2 and L3, the last lenses of the camera. The fixed focus camera re-images the echellogram onto the detector at f/2.1". MSG120 fig. 2: "the pupil mirror re-images the grating on the camera mouth at 2x demagnification".
So: collimator used twice (not three times), prisms in double pass BEFORE and after the echelle, intermediate spectrum near the slit, fold/field mirror, camera that re-images: all as the README says. The prism arrangement of the presets (UVB 60 deg x2 passes, VIS 49 deg SF6 x2, NIR 35 + 22 + 22 in double pass, xPrisms list of six) matches V11. Off-plane angles 2.2 / 2.0 / 1.8 deg match V11. Correct also that this is not the UVES-type white pupil (no separate transfer collimator, cross-disperser before the echelle); the "4c" kind is distinct from "wp" appropriately.

FINDINGS
X1 (minor wording). The caption word "flat" is wrong or unsupported in each arm. NIR: M8 is "a spherical mirror" (V11 Sec. 2.5.3) that acts as a field/pupil mirror with 2x pupil demagnification (MSG120). UVB and VIS: V11 says only "a second folding mirror ... acts also as a field mirror"; a field mirror that relocates the pupil needs power; D06 Sec. 3.2 says the camera optics are 80 mm against a 100 mm collimated beam, a demagnified pupil, so the mirror (or the focal-plane field optics) must carry power (INFERRED; the power of the UVB/VIS fold is not stated in the papers read). Suggest the label "fold and field mirror" and the README phrase "a fold and field mirror (spherical, in the NIR) near the slit re-forms the pupil on the camera; the camera re-images the spectrum" . This matches the app's own effective-camera convention (data/presets_research.md l. 659: "the app's 'camera' is the effective (transfer collimator + dioptric camera) system").
X2 (minor). Per arm: the arrangement is the same (4C) in all three; differences the page does not show: NIR collimator is two mirrors + corrector (M6, M7), UVB/VIS one mirror + corrector; NIR has three prisms (the page does draw "3 prisms, passed twice"); the intermediate spectrum in the NIR is formed by a spherical mirror M8 after a fold M5 ahead of the slit. The preset note calls all three "mirror (Maksutov-type) collimator"; for the NIR it is a "Maksutov-inspired" two-mirror collimator (V11 Sec. 2.5.3; D06: "modified ... through a two-mirrors collimator"). Minor.
X3 (not checked). "below the slit" / sS geometry and the 0.6 L_y spacing are labeled schematic in the caption; fine. Spano 2006, Rasmussen 2008 and Guinouard 2006 were not fetched; the claims are fully covered by V11, D06, MSG120 and Navarro 2008 (Navarro: "The light passes the collimator again, enters the camera and reaches the detector", and "After the double pass" for the cross dispersion, the NIR manuscript, lines 104-127 of the extracted text).

PART 3. Feature 2, double pass (e.dpass: MIKE, M2FS)
======================================================================

Caption (app.js ~l. 582): "Double pass: one lens is both collimator and camera. The returning beam leaves 2 theta ... from the incoming one in this plane ... and passes beside the slit's injection optics to the detector." (In-plane variant; the other variant says the slit sits beside the detector out of the page.)

MIKE: CONFIRMED. B03 Abstract: "calcium fluoride lenses which function as both camera and collimator in a double pass configuration. A single, standard echelle grating is used on each side ... illuminated close to true Littrow. Prism cross-dispersers are also used double-[pass]". B03 Sec. 2.3: "The optical axes of the cameras are offset by about 30mm from the optical axes of the injection optics" and the injection optics "form a virtual image of the slit in the focal plane of each camera and substitute for the field flattener (dewar window) in the first pass through the cameras. The triplet and a flat mirror occupy less than half of the focal plane". So: one lens group serves as collimator (first pass, fed by the injection triplet and flat) and as camera (second pass), the prisms are passed twice (before and after the echelle), the offset between the beams is across the orders (out of the dispersion plane: the preset uses gamma 1.6-1.7 deg with theta 0, consistent with the caption's out-of-the-page variant). The external stop distances 400 mm (red) and 600 mm (blue) are B03 Sec. 2.2 ("designed with an external stop at roughly the spacing to the center of the echelle grating (400mm on the red side, 600mm on the blue side)"): matches layLen. Camera f/3.3 red, f/3.6 blue, 150 mm pupil, 500/550 mm: B03 Sec. 2.2 and Sec. 2.3. Blue "two fused silica prisms with 38 deg apex angles provide the equivalent of a single 62 deg apex angle prism": B03 Sec. 3.

M2FS: CONFIRMED. M12 Sec. 2.3 (spectrograph description): "From the injection mirror, the light then passes through a singlet/triplet lens system ... In HiRes mode, light from the principal optics passes through a cross-dispersion prism (XDP) to a 200 mm x 420 mm R2.6 (69 deg blaze angle) echelle grating ... The dispersed light is reflected back through the XDP a second time and then again through singlet/triplet system--now acting as a camera--passing just below the injection optics. The beams then pass through a field lens and onto a CCD detector". Table 4: camera/collimator focal length 500 mm, f/3.4, beam 147 mm, XDP apex 23.6 deg Schott F2 (the preset uses 500, 3.4, 147, F2, 23.6, 52.67 /mm at 69 deg): all match. The caption's in-plane variant ("passes beside the slit's injection optics") matches "passing just below the injection optics".
- Note the in-plane angle: preset comment says 2 theta = 6.6 deg between beams in the dispersion plane, "set from the measured arc-line dispersion"; M12 Table 4 gives "Quasi-Littrow Opening Angle ~8-10 deg, varies depending on mode/gratings used". The preset value is 20-30% below the published range; the comment should cite this (not an error of the caption).
- The LoRes mode (no XDP, flat grating) is not drawn, correct for the echelle path.

Differences the caption hides (minor): in both instruments the first pass is fed by separate injection optics (MIKE: field lens, flat mirror, injection triplet; M2FS: filter, field lens, injection mirror), and the last element is the field flattener / dewar window ("this last lens also serves as the CCD dewar window"); the one-lens statement is exact for the main group only.
