## Findings, most serious first

**1. fors2hr, pub line "field 4.25′", src "FORS2 manual": WRONG attribution (scored line).**
- The manual prints 4.3′: Table 2.2 reads "Field of View 7.′1×7.′1 (1)  4.′3×4.′3".
- 4.25′ appears only on the ESO FORS overview web page (rawdata/imaging/fors2/eso_fors_overview.html): "... and 4.25' x 4.25' respectively."
- Fix the src to the ESO web page (or score against 4.3′). The model gives 4.27′, so the check passes either way.
- Earlier audit I3 flagged only the field-stop wording of this note, not the attribution.

**2. lbcr note, "No gaps are published for LBC-Red": WRONG wording.**
- Rothberg+16 Sec. 3.1 (data/imaging/vst_lbc.md key R16) says of the LBCs: "Each CCD covers 7′.8 × 17′.6 with a gap of 70 pixels (18″) between each CCD".
- The LBTO focal-plane-layout page (rawdata/imaging/lbc/lbto_scienceops_lbc_focal-plane-layout.html) says: "gaps between the chips of ~18 arcseconds".
- That is 70 px = 0.945 mm at 13.5 µm, and it is stated for both cameras. What does not exist is a Red-specific measurement.
- The preset uses Giallongo's LBC-Blue 1.0 / 1.03 mm (74 and 76 px), which is 6% wider than 0.945 mm.
- Audit I2 (vst_lbc) item 5 recommended the wording "no Red measurement exists". The current text says "no gaps are published".
- Also, the md's recommended preset gap_x is 0.945 mm.

**3. decam note, "Two CCDs are dead or partial (N30, and S30 in 2014–2016; NOIRLab counts 60.5 useful)": partly WRONG, partly UNSUPPORTED.**
- NOIRLab news (noirlab_DECam_main.html): "2013 November 30: CCD S30 is not working" and "2016 December 29: ... CCD S30 began working again!! Since November 2013 ...". The outage was Nov 2013 to Dec 2016, not 2014–2016.
- With S30 revived, only N30 is dead (61 CCDs). So the main page's "62 science CCDs (60.5 useful)" cannot be explained as N30 + S30. The source gives no composition for the 0.5.
- Suggest: "N30 failed in 2012; S30 failed Nov 2013 and recovered Dec 2016; NOIRLab quotes 60.5 useful (breakdown not given)".

**4. niri14 note, "the f/14 camera for imaging in the L and M bands": design intent, contradicted by Gemini's operating pages.**
- Hodapp+03 does say f/14 "allows imaging in the L and M bands".
- Gemini's NIRI intro (rawdata/imaging/niri/gemini_webarchive_NIRIIntro.html) says: "Imaging with all cameras and all filters is possible, with the exceptions of the L' band at f/6 and the M band at f/6 and f/14, where the background saturates the array in the minimum exposure time".
- The current NIRI page table says f/14 "no M band". The same intro says Altair is usable at f/14 "for all imaging except in the M band".
- Say "designed for L and M; M saturates in practice".

**5. niri32: AO f/32 scale and distortion since 2018 (older design vs current configuration).**
- Gemini's NIRI capability page: "NIRI with Altair has been measured to have radial barrel distortion at f/32 ... k = (1.32 ± 0.02) × 10⁻⁵ ... This solution is valid for data taken with f/32 before August 2018. A new solution is being developed after the f/32 mirror shift."
- The preset uses 0.0214″/px and 21.9″, which is the pre-2018 table, and says nothing about the 2018 mirror shift or the distortion.
- The note's "NIRI is not currently offered" is supported (page says "Unavailable For Proposals", "Retired Instruments").

**6. fors2hr (and fors2sr): published beam diameter not mentioned.**
- ESO's FORS description (eso_fors_inst_index.html): collimators "f1233mm (COLL_SR) and f616 mm (COLL_HR) giving a 90 mm and a 44.5 [mm] ..." beam.
- The SR note quotes ESO's 90 mm against the model's 91.9 mm.
- The HR note is silent. The model's W = 45.93 mm is 3.2% above ESO's 44.5 mm.

**7. mosfirei: printed pupil value hidden, and a printed field described as arithmetic.**
- McLean+10 Sec. 3.1: "The telescope's effective focal length is 149.572 m ... effective f/ratio is f/14.50. Thus the collimator's focal length must be 1813.0 mm so as to produce a (stipulated) 125.0-mm beam diameter" and "The 125 mm pupil image ...". The 125 mm design pupil is printed.
- The preset uses W = 132.72 mm, which is 1813/13.66 (DERIVED). data/round4/mosfire.md explains this, but the preset note does not, even though it cites Sec. 3.1 for the 327.5 and 300 mm distances (which are correct there).
- The pub line "field 6.1′ square ... (2040 px × 0.1798″: the same arithmetic)": the field is printed. McLean+12 abstract says "6.1′ × 6.1′" and McLean+10 says "6.14′ x 6.14′ field". The 0.1798″ is also printed ("the measured value is 0.1798″"), so that line is fine.

**8. ztf: orientation conflict between papers not stated.**
- Bellm+19 Table 1: "Chip gaps 0.205° N-S, 0.140° E-W".
- Dekany+20 Table 3: "Inter-detector gaps (active) 0.205° (E-W), 0.140° (N-S)".
- The preset takes Bellm's assignment (gapX 7.49, gapY 10.96 mm). That is the only one that reproduces the measured 7.32° E-W field (Dekany's gives 7.51°).
- The note mentions only the camera-paper mosaic-size conflict. The research record (megacam_ztf_small.md) has the swap.
- Also, "ε is the area-equivalent of the published on-axis obscuration (22.4%)": Bellm says "Low obscuration (22.4% including spiders)" and Dekany "22.4% on-axis, up to ≈46% at field maximum". Neither states area vs linear. ε = √0.224 assumes area (the 46% figure is consistent with area, but this is an unstated convention).

**9. wfc3ir: scored line rests on a convention, plus an unsupported explanation.**
- "pixel scale 0.128″/px (geometric mean of 0.135 × 0.121)", scored at 1%: the handbook prints only 0.135 × 0.121 (Table 2.1) and "~0.1355, ~0.1211". The geometric mean is the preset's convention for a square-pixel model. Because F_cam comes from the CDR's 29,000 mm, which gives 0.1280″ exactly, the match is partly by construction.
- "the detector's 24° tilt about one axis explains ≈ 6% of the 10%": the handbook says "tilted by 24°" and ISR "~7% rhomboidal elongation" (UVIS). I could not find a source for the 6%. It is the preset's own arithmetic.
- The "published f/11" and 29,000 mm are in the CDR (rawdata/imaging/wfc3/WFC3_CDR_Dec2000 ...: "F/11", "OTA + WFC3 focal length = 29,000 mm") and the handbook Table 2.1. Fine.
- The same convention class applies to nircamsw/nircamlw: the scored "in-flight" scale is a geometric-mean-of-XSciScale/YSciScale average over detectors (labeled DERIVED; I reproduced it).
