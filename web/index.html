About the model

Each pair of antennas is a baseline. Seen from the source at hour angle H and declination δ, a baseline samples the sky's visibility at (u, v), the baseline's projection on the plane facing the source, in wavelengths; as the Earth turns, each sample moves along an ellipse, and each sample also gives a mirror point (−u, −v). Samples with the source below the elevation limit are left out. A wide band spreads each sample into a radial streak, one point per frequency, over one contiguous band about the frequency (ALMA's basebands really sit in two sidebands). The antenna positions are the observatories' configuration files (for ALMA, the 43-antenna sets of the current cycle); the made-up layouts use the array's dishes at the array's site.

The M101 sky is the Hubble mosaic heic0602a, in gray, resized to 8000 pixels across (0.099″ a pixel) and turned 3.5° to put north up, with the picture's brightness as the sky's. The weighted samples are gridded and Fourier transformed: the weights give the synthesized (dirty) beam, and the weights times the model sky's visibilities give the dirty image, the sky times the primary beam (a Gaussian of the stated half-power width), convolved with the beam. Uniform and robust weighting count the samples in uv cells about 1/250 of the uv plane's extent. The beam's size is the FWHM of a Gaussian fitted to the beam's main lobe. The model leaves out noise, calibration, the w term of wide fields, the smearing of a sample over the sample's integration time and channel width, and deconvolution (CLEAN).

The telescope, collimator and camera are ideal thin optics, so each star images to a point at f_eff tan θ from the axis; the numbers come from tracing rays through the optics and are checked against the closed forms (plate scale 206265 / f_eff, pupil position, field angle in the beam) in the validation suite. A star's image is the seeing (a Moffat profile, with the diffraction width added in quadrature) or the Airy pattern of the annular aperture; the band sets only the wavelength of the diffraction width. The field of stars and galaxies is synthetic and illustrative.

The collimator and camera are ideal (aberration-free), and the dispersers are plane.

The numbers are measured by tracing rays through the vector grating equation and Snell's law, from the slit to the detector; the closed forms of the design notes (R φ = 2 tan δ W/D in Littrow, Fcam ≥ npix p/(φ D), and the rest) are reproduced by the trace and checked in the validation suite.

The resolving power is the FWHM of the slit image, with the grating's diffraction limit and the pixel width added in quadrature; blaze efficiency is the scalar sinc² envelope, relative to the peak.

Presets come from the instrument papers and manuals. The presets are approximate and are meant to reproduce the optical design of each spectrograph (and so the numbers); the drawing's spacings, folds, and angles do not follow the actual optical layout. Fiber-fed instruments are entered as the slit of equal line width.
